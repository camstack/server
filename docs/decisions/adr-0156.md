## Context

The audio analyser follows the camera's `audioStreamId`, else its motion
profile. When camera 108's motion profile was remapped onto a derived stream
that could not carry audio ([D155](adr-0155.md)), the orchestrator subscribed to
it, logged `Audio stream subscribed`, pulled zero chunks for four days and said
nothing else. Two mechanisms that exist precisely to catch this both stayed
quiet:

- the subscription never asks whether the stream HAS audio — it opens a chunk
  subscription against any broker id;
- the watchdog's `audio` stage arms only when the source is known to carry
  audio, and that evidence was read as `BrokerStats.audio !== null` at DISPATCH
  time, when the broker is normally cold. `getStats()` answers `audio: null`
  until a track is observed, so a cold read disarmed the stage — the watchdog
  had never once watched 618's audio.

`BrokerStats.audio === null` therefore means two different things ("there is no
track" and "I have not looked yet"), and each consumer needs the opposite
default: the subscription must not refuse on a cold read, the watchdog must not
disarm on one.

## Decision

One classifier, `classifyAudioTrackProbe`, turns a `getBrokerStats` answer into
`present` / `absent` / `unknown`, and `absent` requires POSITIVE evidence: a
broker that is `streaming` and has been for `AUDIO_PROBE_SETTLE_MS`, still
reporting no track. A missing `audio` field (version skew) is `unknown`, never
`absent`. Both consumers read that one classifier: the audio subscription
REFUSES an `absent` stream with a single WARN carrying `tags: { deviceId }` and
naming the stream, and the watchdog's audio stage arms unless the answer is
`absent`.

There is **no automatic fallback to another stream.** Which stream carries a
camera's audio is an operator setting; substituting one silently makes "which
stream is my audio on" unanswerable and hides the misconfiguration the WARN
exists to report.

## Consequences

- A camera whose selected stream genuinely has no audio produces one refusal
  line per resubscribe (dispatch, analyser readiness, reconcile) instead of a
  silent no-op — and no chunk subscription is opened against it.
- A refusal is not terminal: the next resubscribe re-reads the probe, so a
  stream that gains audio (a fix deployed, a camera re-dialed) is picked up
  without operator action.
- The watchdog now arms the audio stage in the `unknown` case, so cameras that
  really have no audio can arm a stage nothing feeds. That is bounded by an
  existing rule: a stage that never produced ONE signal is disarmed at recovery
  exhaustion, with one line. Under-arming was the worse failure — it is what
  made a four-day outage invisible.
- The refusal gate costs one `getBrokerStats` RPC per subscribe, and it runs
  before the settings read so a refused camera pays nothing else.

## Enforcement

`audio-track-probe.spec.ts` pins the three-way classification (including that a
missing field is not absence). `audio-subscription-refusal.spec.ts` pins the
refusal, its `tags.deviceId` and stream name, that nothing downstream runs, and
that inconclusive evidence still subscribes. `watchdog-audio-probe.spec.ts`
continues to pin that the evidence is read once per dispatch, from the broker,
for the audio stage's own stream.
