## Context

The operator asked (2026-08-07) for a sixth per-camera switch: the camera's
microphone on or off, at the device. It belongs on the privacy capability —
`privacy-mask` already owns "what this camera deliberately does not capture",
and both planes are applied on the device before anything leaves it.

[D62](adr-0062.md) anticipated new switches ("a new switch is a new
`CameraSwitchAuthority` variant") but its first non-optional consequence does
not survive contact with this one:

> Availability is derived, never hardcoded. `deviceManager.listBindableCapsForDeviceType`
> decides whether a switch is offered at all, and a capability with no
> registered wrapper is not a switch — nothing could apply it.

`privacy-mask` is a device-NATIVE cap. It appears in
`listBindableCapsForDeviceType('camera')` with `wrappers: []` — the enumeration
is per device TYPE and looks only at `def.scope === 'device'`
(`capability-registry.ts:listDeviceScopedCapsForType`) — and the switch
service's own filter, `bindable.filter(b => b.wrappers.length > 0)`, then drops
it. It is likewise invisible to the `kind !== 'wrapped'` filter on
`getBindings`. Applying D62's rule literally offers the microphone switch on
**no camera at all**; inverting the filter offers it on **every** camera,
including models with no microphone, which is the dead-control failure the same
rule exists to prevent. Neither answer is about the camera in front of you.

The survey that preceded this found the other half of the problem. There was no
raw device-settings path left to remove — Reolink and Hikvision both migrated
their stream sections to the `stream-params` cap and both say so in the code —
but `StreamProfilePatchSchema` still carried a per-profile `audio` boolean that
**one** provider honoured, **no** form rendered and **no** caller ever set. A
second writer onto one device register, reachable by nobody: the shape of the
knobs this repo has already shipped that disagreed with the reader.

## Decision

**When a switch's authority is the DEVICE, its availability comes from that
capability's own camera-probed `getOptions`, and the bindings answer is used
only to decide whether to ask.** `getBindings` (all `kind`s, the way
`addon-export-alexa`'s discovery reads it) says whether the cap is bound to
this camera at all; `privacyMask.getOptions().supportsAudioMute` — probed live,
`true` only when the firmware actually returned a flag we know how to patch —
says whether THIS camera can do it. A camera with no binding costs zero round
trips and reports `no-provider`; a camera that would not answer reports
`source-unreachable` and renders no control either way.

This is not a weakening of D62's rule, it is the same rule one level finer:
`listBindableCapsForDeviceType` answers for a device *type*, and a probe
answers for a *camera*.

Two consequences are not optional:

1. **The device's audio flag has exactly one writer.** `audio` was deleted from
   `StreamProfilePatchSchema` and from Reolink's `buildEncStreamPatch`.
   `StreamProfileConfigSchema.audio` survives as a per-profile READ.
2. **A mute writes every profile, verifies every profile, and REVERTS rather
   than half-applying.** Both firmwares store the flag per stream and let the
   streams disagree. `summarisePrivacyAudio` is `some`, not `every`, so a
   camera whose sub stream is still audible reads as ON and is switchable off;
   a camera left half-muted would report silence it does not have.

   This is not hypothetical. Measured live on Reolink 592 (RLC, 2026-08-07):
   `setEnc` moved `mainStream` and `subStream` and left `thirdStream` at 1,
   both when the three were sent in ONE call and when each was sent in its
   own — and the published SDP confirmed it at the consumer layer
   (`native:ext` kept its `m=audio` line while main and sub lost theirs). The
   camera accepts the call and ignores it. So the provider re-reads after
   writing, and if any stream refused it puts the accepted ones BACK and
   throws, naming the stream. A privacy control that half-applies is the one
   outcome worse than an unavailable one: the operator believes the camera is
   silent and it is not.

`CameraStatus.switchedOff` needed no change: it is an array of switch ids, not
a boolean, so a partial function switched off has always been expressible.
`device-audio` simply appears in the list — the camera is not marked off.

## Consequences

- `CameraSwitchAuthority` gains a `camera-audio` variant carrying `capName`.
  The `switch` in `resolveState` and in `applyWrite` is exhaustive, so a future
  device-authority switch cannot be half-wired.
- `getCameraSwitches` costs two extra round trips **only** for cameras that
  have a `privacy-mask` binding. `CameraStatus` pays this once per camera, so
  the ordering — bindings first, camera second — is load-bearing at fleet
  scale, not tidiness.
- The group can express states no server-side store could hold, and can also
  fail in a way the other five cannot: the camera can be unreachable. That is
  why `supported` and `enabled` are separate nullable facts rather than one
  tri-state boolean.
- Providers that implement `privacy-mask` must now answer `supportsAudioMute`
  and `audioEnabled`. Both are required fields, so a provider that forgets does
  not compile. Every fallback/cold-start descriptor answers `false` / `null`:
  a guessed `true` would render a switch on a camera nobody asked.
- Not implemented, surveyed and recorded rather than assumed: **amcrest** can
  do this today through the `Encode[0].MainFormat[0].AudioEnable` key its
  existing `setConfig` already writes; **onvif** could, via
  `add/removeAudioEncoderConfiguration` in the bundled lib, but has no client
  method and no `stream-params` cap; **wyze** cannot without a `set_property`
  call that does not exist upstream; **rtsp** cannot, having no control channel
  at all; unifi/tuya/homeassistant are not camera providers here.

## Enforcement

- `packages/types/src/interfaces/__tests__/camera-switches.spec.ts` — the pure
  derivation: probe-derived availability, `no-provider` vs
  `source-unreachable` kept distinct, and a supported-but-unreadable camera
  never rendered as a control defaulted to on.
- `packages/addon-pipeline-orchestrator/src/__tests__/camera-switch-service.spec.ts`
  — the fake reports `privacy-mask` as `kind: 'native'` with `wrappers: []`,
  which is the exact shape the wrapper filters drop, so a derivation that read
  the bindable list cannot pass. Also pins that a camera without the binding is
  asked NOTHING, and that the microphone write touches no other authority.
- `packages/addon-provider-hikvision/src/__tests__/hikvision-stream-audio.spec.ts`
  — the one place a bug would be silent and expensive: every fixture carries
  both the channel-level `<enabled>` and `<Audio><enabled>`, so a first-match
  read or patch fails. Patching the wrong one takes the camera's video stream
  down while the operator believes they muted a microphone.
- The exhaustive `switch` over `CameraSwitchAuthority` is the compiler's share.
  There is **no guard script** for "the group stores nothing" — same as D62, it
  rests on the record and on review.
