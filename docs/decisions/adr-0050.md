## Context

`addon-export-hap` advertised HomeKit Secure Video by passing a `recording`
option into the `CameraController`, backed by a stub delegate. The stub's
`handleRecordingStreamRequest` was a `require-yield` generator: it logged the
request and returned, without ever yielding a `RecordingPacket` and therefore
without ever setting `RecordingPacket.isLast`.

That is a contract violation, and hap-nodejs said so on every single request:

```
export-hap: HKSV stream requested — stub returns empty (no clip source yet) {streamId=1}
[HDS ...] Delegate finished streaming for 1 without setting RecordingPacket.isLast.
export-hap: HKSV stream closed {streamId=1 reason=3}
```

Twelve seconds elapsed between each request and its close — the controller was
waiting out a timeout. This ran every 20-60 s, all day, on every camera bound to
`motion-detection` (the gate the stub used).

The stub existed for a UI affordance: advertising `recording` gives iOS Home the
Activity tab and the per-camera "Stream and Allow Recording" settings, so an
operator could flip the toggle without HomeKit complaining the camera lacked the
service. **Keeping it for that reason was the rejected alternative.** It buys a
settings screen whose every action fails, and it costs a permanent contract
violation plus a `CameraOperatingMode` service carrying `HomeKitCameraActive` —
a plausible, untested coupling to live view that nobody could rule out while the
stub was there.

The other alternative, **satisfy the contract properly**, is not available: a
conforming generator must emit a `MEDIA_INITIALIZATION` fMP4 packet followed by
`MEDIA_FRAGMENT` packets and mark the last one, and there is no fragment source
to build them from until the recorder grows a fragment-export surface. A
generator that yields a fabricated empty initialisation segment would be the
same lie in a longer form.

## Decision

An exporter advertises a HAP service only when it can honour that service's
contract. `recording` is optional in `CameraControllerOptions`; omitting it
removes `CameraRecordingManagement` and `CameraOperatingMode` entirely, which is
the supported way to say "this camera does not record". Do not re-add
`recording` without a delegate that yields real fragments and sets `isLast`.

The general rule: **a stub that registers and then misbehaves is worse than not
registering.** It presents to the operator as a working feature, it produces a
contract-violation log loop that trains readers to ignore the addon's logs, and
it adds services whose side effects nobody has tested.

## Consequences

- iOS Home loses the Activity tab and the per-camera recording settings for
  CamStack cameras. Recording and playback continue to live in the CamStack UI
  and the viewer, which is where the byte-range read path (D31) actually works.
- Re-pairing is not required; the services simply stop being published on the
  next accessory rebuild.
- Whether the stub also degraded **live** view remains `[undetermined]` — it was
  never tested, and this change removes the possibility rather than answering it.
  Do not claim the live stream was fixed by this.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-export-hap/src/mappers/__tests__/camera-accessory-recording.spec.ts`
builds a real `Accessory` through `buildCameraAccessory` with `motion-detection`
bound — the exact gate the stub used — and asserts neither
`CameraRecordingManagement` nor `CameraOperatingMode` is present, plus that the
live camera and motion services still are. Restoring the stub turns the first two
red (verified).
