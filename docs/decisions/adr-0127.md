## Context

An export's ffmpeg filter sees `t`. `t` is NOT wall time: it runs over the
export's source playlist, which is the concatenation of the segment files that
EXIST for the range, so every recording gap is simply absent from it. Measured
here, on device 101: a 10 h window (36,000 s) assembled from 2,957 segments held
29,393 s of footage — 6,607 s of holes — and a two-segment probe across one hole
confirmed the concatenated timeline is exactly the sum of the segment
durations (6.031 s + 11.990 s → last pts 17.98 s), with the wall gap nowhere in
it.

The POST-HOC timelapse plans its dense ranges from TRACKS, and a track knows
`firstSeen` — a wall instant. Those seconds were handed to `between(t,…)`
verbatim. Every range past the first hole was late by the gap accumulated before
it: 10 s after the first, ~1 h 50 m by dawn, i.e. past EOF. Nothing matched. The
delivered video was a uniform timelapse and the log line printed
`denseRanges=5` — the count that had been ASKED for, so the log read as
verification and the video was never probed (export `57d14363`, 2026-08-13).

The alternative considered and rejected: make the PLANNER emit stream seconds.
It cannot. Producing them requires each segment's `(startMs, durMs)` and their
cumulative offsets, which exist only inside the recorder, only for the profile
that was actually chosen, and only at the moment the playlist is written.

## Decision

A range handed to a render is stated in WALL time relative to the export's own
`fromMs`, and the component that BUILDS THE SOURCE PLAYLIST translates it onto
ffmpeg's clock before argv is built. The map is the monotone step function
"footage that precedes this wall instant": a range spanning a gap is CLIPPED to
the recorded parts (which are contiguous in stream time — a gap occupies zero of
it, so a clip never splits), a range inside a gap or past the footage is
DROPPED, and every render carrying an overlay logs `denseRangesRequested` vs
`denseRangesMapped` with the dropped ranges and their reason, tagged with the
`deviceId`. A drop is never silent.

## Consequences

- `resolvePlaylist` answers `{ path, segments }`, not a path. The timeline it
  encodes cannot be recovered later — the playlist file is deleted the moment
  the render ends.
- The cap shape is unchanged: `ExportDenseRange.fromSec/toSec` still mean
  seconds from `fromMs`, now documented as the wall clock they always were. The
  fix therefore rides an addon deploy, with no release train.
- The mapping never grows the list, so a caller that respected
  `EXPORT_DENSE_MAX_RANGES` cannot be pushed past the ceiling by it.
- Residual error: the map uses the INDEX's nominal `durMs`, and a segment
  finalized right after a writer restart can carry a rounded duration (11,000 ms
  for 11,990 ms of content). Measured over one night that is ~40 s of drift in
  29,393 s (0.13%) — against the ±5 s pad on each range, tolerable at the start
  of a window and worth re-measuring if a range ever misses near its end.
- An overlay that maps to NOTHING is removed entirely rather than left with an
  empty range list: `ExportDenseSchema` refuses `ranges: []` and the predicate
  would emit `if(,…)`, which fails the render instead of losing a cadence.

## Enforcement

`export-dense-map.spec.ts` (identity on a gapless window, clip across a gap,
drop-in-gap, drop-past-end, anchor on the first segment, never grows the list)
and two `export-engine.spec.ts` cases that assert the ARGV carries stream-time
ranges and that the log line reports requested vs mapped. Neutralising the map
(returning the wall offsets) turns 9 of the 13 unit cases red.

Live: probe the VIDEO, never the log. The OSD timestamps of consecutive output
frames are the ground truth — 60 s apart at the base cadence, 2 s apart inside a
dense section.
