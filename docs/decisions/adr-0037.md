## Context

The hub had two inference devices enabled — `openvino:gpu` and `openvino:npu`,
both `available`, both weight 1, neither capped, no camera carrying a device
pin. Every session ran on the iGPU. The NPU hardware was present
(`/dev/accel/accel0`, `intel_vpu` loaded, visible inside the container) and the
root-capability guard could not separate the two (both are `openvino`, so both
pass or both fail).

The first suspicion was the guard, then the pin, then the probe. All three were
innocent. The cause was the last line of `balance()`:

```ts
.toSorted((a, b) => a.sortKey - b.sortKey || a.node.nodeId.localeCompare(b.node.nodeId))[0]
```

Sessions on this deployment are ON-MOTION: they start, run for seconds, and end,
and they essentially never overlap (`getLocalLoad` reports `attachedCameras: 1`).
So at every dispatch both devices score 0, the weights are equal, and the
**lexicographic tie-break decides every dispatch** — `openvino:gpu` <
`openvino:npu`, forever. The balancer balances CONCURRENT load, and this cluster
is almost never concurrent. A July log showing 77 sessions on the NPU against 8
on the iGPU was not evidence that it once worked correctly; it was evidence that
the test traffic that day happened to be concurrent.

Nothing logged any of this. Not the eligible set, not the chosen device, not the
reason, and not the three places `resolveEligibleInferenceDevices` silently drops
a device (`!enabled`, `!available`, root guard). Distinguishing "the balancer
keeps choosing the GPU" from "the NPU is not even a candidate" took two hours of
re-deriving eligibility by hand.

## Decision

At the DEVICE tier, a genuine tie — two devices the capacity signal cannot
separate — is resolved fairly, by round-robin across the tied set, keyed by the
tied set itself. At the NODE tier the tie-break stays the constant
`nodeId.localeCompare`. `balance()` therefore remains a pure, deterministic
function and gains no rotation, no clock and no hidden state; the fair choice
lives entirely in `device-balancer.ts`, which re-ranks the same input via
`rankCandidates()` to see whether the winner actually won or merely sorted first.

Fairness never overrides correctness: the operator's device pin is still L1 and
still wins, `maxSessions` caps still remove a device from the candidate set
before any tie exists, and a camera that is ALREADY placed on one of the tied
devices keeps it (`currentDeviceKey`) so a re-dispatch is idempotent and never
thrashes a live camera between accelerators. The on-motion path deliberately
passes no `currentDeviceKey` — an ephemeral session has no standing placement,
and treating the previous session's device as sticky would re-create exactly
this bug.

Every dispatch emits one line naming the eligible set, the chosen key and the
reason; every shrinkage of the candidate set names the dropped device and why.

## Consequences

- The node tier's "equal-share re-runs don't reshuffle" property is unchanged,
  which is why the rotation could not simply be pushed into `balance()`.
- The rotation is mutable state in the orchestrator process. It is advisory: a
  lost cursor (restart) costs at most one unfair dispatch. It must never be
  consulted for anything but a tie.
- A decayed-history score was rejected: it needs a clock (untestable without
  injection), needs a decay constant, and a half-life longer than the
  inter-session gap silently collapses back into the original bug.
- Two log lines per dispatch path now exist and must stay bounded to
  per-dispatch — never per frame.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-pipeline-orchestrator/src/__tests__/device-balancer.spec.ts`
(unit: rotation, stickiness, pin precedence, cap precedence) and
`inference-device-selection.spec.ts` (end-to-end through
`handleSessionMotion` → `dispatchDetectionSession`: the rotation is WIRED, the
line is emitted, and it carries `tags.deviceId`). The exclusion reporting is
gated by `node-inference-devices-cap.spec.ts`. Each of those gates was removed
and confirmed to fail before being restored.
