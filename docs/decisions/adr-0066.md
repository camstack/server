## Context

HomeKit live view started rendering on 2026-08-06 after a year. H.264 cameras
pass through and appear immediately; the one H.265 camera (112) **must**
transcode — iOS Home renders only H.264 over the classic HAP SRTP path — and it
renders but takes a long time to start.

The join burst was ruled out: its prime line reads `source=keyframe-only
packets=1 spanMs=0 withheld=0`. What is left is the transcode itself, and it was
pure software on both halves — `libx264 -preset ultrafast` with **no
decoder-side flag at all**. An independent measurement put a 4K→1080p transcode
at **77 % of one core, dominated by DECODE**, on a hub already measured at
70-95 % main-thread with 42 s of synchronous blocks per 120 s.

The hub is an Intel Core Ultra 7 265K with an iGPU, so `-hwaccel qsv` is the
obvious lever — and it is the wrong one twice over.

- A flag naming a device the node does not have does not degrade the stream, it
  **kills ffmpeg at spawn**. The fleet also carries an Apple-silicon node and an
  N100. A hardcoded backend turns a working camera into a blank tile, which is
  indistinguishable from the failure this path just escaped.
- On this exact hardware `qsv` is the backend that does NOT work. The decoder
  addon's `DECODE_HWACCEL_RANK` puts `vaapi` above `qsv` deliberately, because
  qsv children exit `code=171` producing no frames while vaapi does 8 MP h264 at
  ~50 fps. `ctx.kernel.hwaccel.resolve()` — the raw platform probe — returns
  `['qsv','vaapi']` for an Intel host, i.e. the broken one first.

## Decision

The HAP transcode's decode backend comes from `decoder.getInfo`, node-pinned to
the local node: the operator's explicit `hwaccel` when it names a backend,
otherwise the addon's own `probedBestHwaccel@<node>`. It is never chosen locally,
never defaulted, and never taken from `ctx.kernel.hwaccel.resolve()` — an
unreadable, unprobed or unrecognised answer decodes in **software**, logged with
a named reason. Hardware **encode** is not enabled. If a hardware decode child
dies inside the init window having forwarded no video and without a controller
stop, the identical argument vector is respawned once without the decode flags.

## Consequences

- The HomeKit path and the pipeline cannot disagree about what a node's hardware
  is; both read the same per-node value, and an operator who turns hardware
  decode off in the decoder settings turns it off here too.
- A node with no decoder addon bound pays full software decode. That is the safe
  direction and it is said out loud once per session rather than inferred.
- **Encode stays on `libx264`, and this is the load-bearing half.** Two of the
  four causes of the year-long failure were an absent `-g` (x264's 250-frame
  default meant one IDR every 25 s at the 10 fps we dial) and absent in-band
  SPS/PPS (the RTP muxer's `AVFMT_GLOBALHEADER` puts them in extradata, and HAP
  carries no SDP to deliver them). `h264_vaapi` / `h264_qsv` carry their own
  rate-control model, reject `-profile:v baseline`, and emit parameter sets on
  their own schedule. Enabling them would put both defects back in play with no
  hardware here to prove they still hold. Decode is where the measured cost is
  and it changes nothing about the bitstream iOS receives.
- The argument vector moved out of `camera-streams.ts` into
  `stream-ffmpeg-args.ts` so `-hwaccel`'s POSITION is assertable. That position
  is the trap: ffmpeg accepts `-hwaccel` after `-i` without complaint and then
  decodes in software anyway — the cost stays, the log says hardware.
- **Expected saving, not measured here.** The decode share of a 4K→1080p
  session, which the one available measurement puts at most of 77 % of a core.
  Nothing in this repo can measure the post-change figure: it needs the live hub,
  one H.265 session, and `export-hap: video decode path resolved — HARDWARE`
  in the log to confirm the backend was even dialled.

## Enforcement

Three unit specs under
`packages/addon-export-hap/src/mappers/builders/__tests__/`:
`stream-hwaccel.spec.ts` (selection and the fallback predicate),
`stream-ffmpeg-args.spec.ts` (the flags land before `-i`; the software retry is
the same vector minus exactly the decode flags; `-g` and `-bsf:v dump_extra`
survive), and the existing `stream-bitrate.spec.ts` for the encode flags.

Discriminating checks: moving `...decodeArgs` after `-i` turns 1 test red;
replacing the software fallback with an unconditional hardware pick and
`shouldRetryInSoftware` with `return true` turns 7 red.

Not enforced: that the chosen backend actually initialises on a given host.
Nothing but the host can answer that, which is why the runtime fallback exists.
