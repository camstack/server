## Context

Third pass on the same complaint. [D150](./adr-0150.md) stopped the drag parking
(the receiver credit was counted per frame and repaid per push). [D158](./adr-0158.md)
aimed the speculative read where the drag was going instead of at the fragment
next door. The operator still called the scrub slow, against a direct
comparison: the reference NVR on the same camera reads like ≥20 fps where this did
~3.

The census had already said where the time goes, and it is not subtle:
`tickMsP50 = 270` of which `readMsP50 = 263`, `locateMsP50 = 1`,
`demuxMsP50 = 1`, `ackMsP50 = 23`. **The tick is the read.** What the read was:
a GOP byte range — a 2 KB tail probe for the `mfra` index, the `ftyp`+`moov`
head, then the fragment — three random seeks on an array that is also absorbing
every camera's writes, to fetch ~135 KB of the ~230 KB the whole 10 s `low`
segment weighs.

We then reverse-engineered the shipped the reference NVR bundle. Its storage format
is not ours and we are not adopting it (a `.rtsp` file is raw interleaved RTP,
unplayable by anything standard; our fMP4 segments are a deliberate,
load-bearing property). But three of its properties do transfer, and the
measurements above say they are the ones that matter: **it reads the low-res
track only, it seeks with one `createReadStream({start})`, and it holds the
located segment and its index in RAM so a repeat costs no syscall at all.**

The insight that reframes our own numbers: paying three seeks to avoid reading
95 KB more is a bad trade on a spindle, and it buys nothing reusable — the next
tick inside the same ten seconds paid all three again. Bytes are nearly free
here; seeks are the entire cost.

One more piece of field evidence shaped the second half of this record: on a
disk at 98% busy, speculation issued 54 reads for 9 hits. Read-ahead is a bet
that spare I/O exists. When it does not, a prefetch is not free work — it is
another random seek queued ahead of the read the operator is waiting on.

## Decision

**The scrub read unit is the whole segment, for the scrub profile.** A `low`
segment is read in one sequential `readBytes`, demuxed once, and held as access
units; every later tick that lands inside those ten seconds is served from RAM
with no locate, no read and no demux. Everything else keeps the targeted GOP
byte range of [D31](./adr-0031.md) — a `high` segment is ~5 MB, and reading it
whole to push one keyframe would be the same bad trade made backwards. The fork
is the profile, plus a byte ceiling so an unusually fat substream falls back
rather than surprising us.

**Entering scrub warms the window the drag is about to move through**: 90 s back
and 30 s forward from the entry playhead (≈12 segments, ≈2.8 MB at `low`),
walked segment by segment through `locate` so the SPAN is what is configured,
not a segment count. Asymmetric because the gesture is — a drag that starts at
the live edge is looking for what already happened. It is fire-and-forget and
never gates a tick: a cold cache costs the first tick exactly its own read.
It runs only for the profile whose unit is the whole segment.

**Speculation stands down on evidence.** The median of the last few read times
crossing `SCRUB_SLOW_READ_MS` (600 ms) suspends both the prefetch and the warm,
counted as `prefetchSuppressed`, and lifts by itself as soon as reads recover.
Median and not mean, and never on fewer than three samples, so one slow read
cannot suppress a whole drag.

**The cache budget is per profile**: `SCRUB_FRAGMENT_CACHE_MB = 24` for the
scrub profile — ~100 whole `low` segments, i.e. 15–17 minutes of timeline
resident per session — and 2 MB for every other profile, with per-profile LRU
eviction so a heavy profile can never evict the segments a drag is running on.

## Consequences

- A careful drag inside one segment pays ONE read and then nothing: the ceiling
  becomes the coalescing window (50 ms ⇒ ~20 push/s), which is the target.
  A drag crossing minutes pays one sequential read per 10 s of timeline.
- `wholeReads` on the census stops being the version-skew degradation counter
  and becomes the normal path for `low`. Both counters still exist and are still
  distinguishable, which is how the profile fork stays auditable.
- 24 MB per session is a fleet cost of `24 MB × concurrent timeline viewers`.
  The knob is documented at its definition; the effect of lowering it shows up
  as `coverCache` falling.
- Travel stills are now deduped by KEYFRAME, not by cache entry. With a GOP as
  the unit those were the same thing; with a whole segment, deduping by entry
  silently collapsed a ten-second fling into one still while two more sat
  demuxed in RAM.
- The warm and the prefetch share one suppression rule and one concurrency
  bound. If the array is healthy this spends ~3 MB of reads per drag entry; if
  it is not, it spends nothing and says so.
- NOT taken, and still the next lever if this is not enough: `readGopBytes`
  keeps no memo of the parsed `mfra` index or the `ftyp`+`moov` head across
  calls, and `seededIndex` only covers segments this recorder process finalized
  in the last 26 h. That path now matters only for non-`low` profiles.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/recorded/__tests__/scrub-segment-unit.spec.ts`
reproduces both measured regimes (26 s steps and 1.2 s steps, 10 s segments, 4 s
GOPs) and pins: one read per segment and demux once per read, no GOP range for
`low`, the GOP range retained for `high`, the asymmetric warm window, the warm
never gating the first tick, suppression under slow reads and its absence under
fast ones, and the keyframe-level travel dedupe. `scrub-fragment-cache.spec.ts`
pins the per-profile budgets and that a heavy profile cannot evict the scrub
profile's entries. Each was verified to fail with its fix removed — including
the warm, which an existing test caught running on a `high` profile where it
would have read ~60 MB.
