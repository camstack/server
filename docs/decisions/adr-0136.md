## Context

A STATIONARY object is counted only while the stationary registry holds it, and
that registry rebuilds FROM MOTION. After a restart a parked car is therefore
invisible to every occupancy rule until something moves in front of it — on a
quiet driveway, indefinitely. The periodic occupancy re-check is the only thing
that looks at a still scene, and it was OFF by default, so the feature's most
ordinary subject (a car that is simply there) was the one it could not see.

Off was not arbitrary. Each re-check cycle re-subscribes a detection session,
which is an RTSP re-dial, and at the old 30 s default that is 120 re-dials an
hour per camera — a documented pull-decoder churn source.

## Decision

`occupancyRecheckEnabled` defaults to TRUE and `occupancyRecheckSec` defaults to
300 s. The cost of the switch is paid on the INTERVAL, not by leaving the switch
off. The per-camera override still wins in both directions.

## Consequences

~12 re-dials an hour per camera at worst, against 120 at the old interval — and
less in practice, because the timer only fires while `occupancyArmed`, which is
set when a session closes with detections still present and cleared after two
consecutive empty bursts. A parked car is counted within five minutes of a
restart instead of never.

600 s was rejected, not preferred-but-unreachable: `occupancyRecheckSec` is
capped at 300 in the cap schema, and raising the cap is TRAIN-BOUND rather than
addon-bound — the host validates `attachCamera` against ITS copy of the schema,
so a runner asked for 600 would be rejected by the hub until a `@camstack/server`
carrying the wider bound is installed everywhere. 300 is the widest value an
addon deploy can actually ship.

There are two defaults for this field and they must agree: the cap's
`z.boolean().default()` / UI-field `default`, and the orchestrator's
`resolveDetectionSettings` fallback. The EFFECTIVE one is the orchestrator's —
`flat` is hydrated from the schema that addon contributes and the value the
runner receives is the one resolved there — which is also why flipping it ships
with an orchestrator deploy and no train. Both were flipped together.

## Enforcement

`device-detection-settings.spec.ts` pins the resolver's default in both
directions. Nothing checks that the two defaults agree; they are adjacent in the
same commit and both are named in the other's comment.
