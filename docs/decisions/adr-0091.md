## Context

The operator received a real doorbell notification on 2026-08-08 and reported
two defects. They are one defect.

**The video started after the event.** [D85](./adr-0085.md) had, the night
before, moved the notification mp4 onto an ON-DEMAND fMP4 seam: an ffmpeg
spawned at the moment the outbox drain asks for footage. That moment was
measured on the live hub — `eventToDialMs=7054` — and ffmpeg discards up to the
first key frame it can open on, so the attached clip covered
**[event+7 s, event+13 s]**. D85 recorded this consequence honestly and queued a
warm plane as the fix.

**The gif was "de-pixellated".** The gif came from the other path, the broker's
clip ring, whose renderer picks the CHEAPEST assigned rendition
(`pickClipProfile`) — on this fleet a 640×360 substream — and re-encodes it to
480 px at 5 fps at 2× speed. The obvious diagnosis is a missing
`palettegen`/`paletteuse` pair. **It was not missing**: that filter has been in
the ring renderer since [D32](./adr-0032.md) and it is correct. Measured on 101:
same filter, same encoder, fed the 720p rendition at 640 px instead of the 360p
one at 480 px, and the result is a gif nobody calls pixellated (324 KB, 0.50 s
to encode).

So one notification carried two renders, from two sources, of two different
windows, at two resolutions. **The quality complaint and the timing complaint
are the same bug wearing two hats.**

**Three designs were built or costed before this one, and the first two were
wrong in ways worth recording.**

- **A permanent warm fMP4 child per camera with a clip rule.** Fully designed:
  a rule-driven reconcile, a prebuffer ring, cooldowns. Priced from
  [D84](./adr-0084.md) at 0.7 % of a core and ~30 MB RSS per camera, forever. It
  was thrown away on one discovery: **the broker already retains the prebuffer**
  (`StreamBroker.clipBuffer`, 25 s of AnnexB, armed per camera, filled
  opportunistically while something else holds the stream up). Paying a
  permanent ffmpeg per camera to obtain a ring already sitting in RAM is not a
  trade-off, it is a duplicate.
- **Making the RTSP restream prebuffer-aware at join.** Rejected on a fact D85
  measured rather than on taste: `RtspRestreamer` bursts the current GOP to a
  joining session and *nothing from before the spawn survives into the file*.
  Every consumer would still carry its own ffmpeg, and "the past, then live"
  over an RTSP session is a semantics nobody wants to own.
- **Fixing the two renders in the consumer.** The notification centre could
  simply ask the ring twice with identical parameters. That works — it is what
  the fallback below does — but it makes coherence a discipline every future
  consumer (a clips API, a retrain export, an event export) has to keep, and the
  discipline had already been broken once.

## Decision

**`streamBroker.produceEventMedia` is THE production point for event media, and
it produces ONE window in every container the caller asks for.** The broker is
the only process that owns the retained packets, so it is where a cut belongs.
It waits out `postSeconds`, selects the window out of its own ring — which
reaches BACKWARDS from the event with nothing spawned and nothing kept warm —
muxes the mp4 once, and DERIVES any gif from that mp4's bytes. Two attachments
of one notification can no longer disagree, because the consumer never chose two
sources.

**The video is COPIED whenever nothing is being changed.** `clipCanCopy`: mp4,
H.264, real time, and a requested width at or above the source's own. Anything
else re-encodes — a downscale, a playback rate, and H.265 deliberately, because
an mp4 attachment has to play on the phone it lands on. The copy branch is what
makes a notification clip the camera's own picture instead of `libx264 -crf 28`.

**An event production prefers the LARGEST H.264 rendition at or below 1080p**
(`pickBestClipProfile`) — the opposite default to `renderPreBufferClip`'s
cheapest, which was right when an attachment was a 480 px thumbnail with motion
and is the whole "de-pixellated" report now. `renderPreBufferClip` keeps its own
default; it has other callers.

**Artifacts are returned by HANDLE, never inline** ([D9](./adr-0009.md),
[D18](./adr-0018.md)). Every consumer is in another runner. The handle carries
`nodeId` and `fetchEventMedia` must be called pinned to it — `stream-broker` is
a singleton cap and an unpinned call never leaves the hub, so a handle minted on
an agent's broker would be redeemed against the hub's store and come back
`null`. Same contract, same field name and the same reason as
`FrameHandle.nodeId`.

**The clip ring is DEMOTED to a declared fallback and it is not removed.** It
serves a hub whose `@camstack/server` predates the method, an H.265-only camera,
and a moment the production refuses. On that path both formats are asked for
with IDENTICAL parameters — same instant, same rolls, same profile — because a
gif-only rule must keep working and three of the four live rules on this install
are gif-only. That is coherence by discipline where the production has coherence
by construction, and the log line says which one served.

**D85's on-demand seam is deleted.** It produced the best-looking mp4 of the
three and the wrong six seconds of video, and a source that is better in
isolation but incoherent with the gif beside it is worse than the ring.

## Consequences

- **This ships on the framework train.** A new cap method means `npm run
  codegen`, a published `@camstack/server` and `applyServerUpdate`; deploying
  `addon-pipeline` and `addon-post-analysis` alone exposes nothing. Until the
  hub has it, the fallback serves — which is why the fallback must keep serving
  gifs.
- **Nothing idles.** No warm child, no permanent session, no per-camera cost.
  The production costs one ffmpeg mux (plus one gif encode, measured at ~0.5 s)
  for as long as the window, and only when a notification asks.
- **The clip has NO AUDIO.** The broker's AnnexB clip ring retains video
  packets, so a produced mp4 is silent where D85's seam clip carried in-band
  AAC. That is a real regression against yesterday and it was taken knowingly:
  the window is worth more than the audio. Closing it means retaining audio
  packets in the clip ring and muxing a second input — a broker change, not a
  second fragmenter.
- **The pre-roll is bounded by what the ring HELD**, not by what was asked for.
  The ring is 25 s and fills only while something else keeps the camera dialed,
  so a camera nobody watches yields a shorter clip. `coverage.{fromTs,toTs}` is
  returned precisely so a caller can state that rather than imply it, and the
  production log line carries `startOffsetMs` — negative means a real pre-roll.
- **An artifact lives 5 minutes and the store is capped at 64 MB**, oldest-first,
  never evicting the artifact just stored. A handle redeemed late returns `null`
  and says so; a consumer that crashed pins nothing.
- **Two paths still exist and that is the cost of the fallback.** A notification
  can be served by a production or by two ring renders, and only the log line
  distinguishes them. The road to one path is in
  `docs/design/event-media-production.md`.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/__tests__/event-media-production.spec.ts`
covers the profile preference (including the H.265 refusal and the
above-the-ceiling fallback), every branch of `clipCanCopy`, the copy branch's
argv carrying no filter graph, the palette pair and the fps-before-setpts order,
the wall-clock coverage arithmetic, and the store's TTL, eviction order and
never-evict-the-newest bound.
`packages/addon-post-analysis/src/notification-center/event-media/__tests__/`
covers the single production request, the node-pinned redemption, the reported
offsets, and every fallback path — including that the fallback gives both ring
calls identical parameters and that a gif-only rule still gets its gif.

Each gate was verified by DELETING it and watching the matching test go red: the
`speed !== 1` copy guard, the largest-rendition sort, the `size > 1` eviction
bound, and the fallback's parameter identity.
