## Context

`nodeDecodeBudgetMb` (8192) and `maxLiveDecodeWorkers` (8) are cluster
settings applied to every node. The 8192 was the 64 GB hub's number; on
the 16 GB Mac agent, which the balancer loads most (`detectWeight` 70),
eight workers at the measured RSS p90 of 952 MB are 7.6 GB on a machine
already at 80% — a swap away from a stall — while the hub leaves most of
its RAM idle. The operator asked whether this was a performance risk or
useless overload: it is both, in opposite directions on the two nodes.

## Decision

Each runner resolves its own limits (`resolveNodeDecodeLimits`): the
node's agent settings (`decodeBudgetMb`, `maxLiveDecodeWorkers`, set with
`pipelineOrchestrator.setAgentDecodeLimits`) win; otherwise the cluster
value when the operator moved it off the shipped default (a value equal to
the default reads as "nobody chose", the native-lease rule); otherwise half
the node's RAM clamped to 2–16 GB. The node entry is read on the lease-knob
cadence (`NodeDecodeLimitsSource`, 30 s TTL, never a warm copy — D224) and
the admission resizes on the next dial, with one log line naming the
source. The cluster setting stays as the fallback and its description says
so.

## Consequences

A node sized by the operator obeys that size; an untouched cluster keeps
the hub at 16 GB and the Mac at 8 GB, and the count cap still bounds both.
Not built: the admin-UI control for the two per-node fields — the cap
method exists and the placement board is the place for it.

## Enforcement

`node-decode-budget.spec.ts` (precedence and clamps),
`node-decode-limits-source.spec.ts` (TTL, change log, failure keeps the last
value), `agent-settings-cap.spec.ts` (persist and clear). Wiring by type.
