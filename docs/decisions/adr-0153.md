## Context

Tier B (D140) declared native Home Assistant platforms with sub-controls: a
`climate` with a fan mode and a preset, a `water_heater` whose STATE is its
operation mode, a `vacuum` with a fan speed, a `media_player` with a source, a
`lock` with a latch. Every one of those controls was written into
`NATIVE_CAP_PLATFORMS` and every one of them resolved to nothing, because
`resolveBinding` asks the DEGRADED descriptor whether it is `writable` — and
those six descriptors were marked read-only.

They were marked read-only for a good reason that had been generalised one
step too far: their vocabularies are free-form (`availableFanModes`,
`availableSources`, `availableFanSpeeds` live on the slice, not in an enum), so
a static `select` would offer values the device rejects while hiding the ones
it takes. That argument is about the **degraded platform**, not about whether
the value can be written — `setFanMode`, `setOperationMode`, `setFanSpeed`,
`selectSource` and `lockControl.open` have all existed since their caps were
written.

The result was a native climate with no fan mode, a native water heater that
could not change its own state, and a native lock with no latch — Tier B's own
note said these controls "light up on their own once routed", and nothing
routed them.

## Decision

**`writable` on a `CAP_ENTITY_MAP` descriptor means "`CAP_COMMAND_ROUTES`
writes exactly this value". It says nothing about whether Home Assistant can
render a control for it.** Whether a `command_topic` is announced is decided
by the PLATFORM, through `COMMANDABLE_PLATFORMS`: a `sensor` or a
`binary_sensor` never gets one.

So a free-form vocabulary is a `writable` descriptor whose degraded platform
is a `sensor` — a reading in a degraded install, a control on the native
entity — and the D62 rule it appears to bend is intact: no control is ever
announced that writes nothing.

## Consequences

- Six controls that existed only as table rows now work: `climate-control`'s
  fan mode and preset, `fan-control`'s preset, `humidifier`'s mode,
  `water-heater`'s operation mode, `vacuum-control`'s fan speed,
  `media-player`'s source, and `lock-control`'s latch.
- **`setTargetRange` is routed by reading before it writes.** It is the one cap
  method that takes two values at once while Home Assistant sends the two
  edges on two topics. Resolution stays pure — it yields a
  `climate-target-edge` carrying the half it was given — and `applyCapCommand`
  reads `climateControl.getStatus` for the other half. A device reporting
  `null` for it is REFUSED rather than defaulted: substituting the single
  `target` would collapse the band to a point.
- A reviewer can no longer read `writable: true` on a `sensor` row as a bug.
  That is the cost, and it is why the flag's meaning is written down here.

## Enforcement

- `__tests__/command-routes.spec.ts` walks `CAP_COMMAND_ROUTES` and fails on
  any route with no test case; the two `setTargetRange` edges are named in an
  explicit `TESTED_SEPARATELY` list with the reason.
- `__tests__/entity-catalog.spec.ts` still asserts both directions between
  `writable` and the route table: a writable descriptor with no route fails,
  and a route with no descriptor fails.
- `scripts/check-export-coverage.ts` now checks **every `extras[*].field`**
  against the cap's status schema, not just each capability's primary field.
  Those are the fields the new routes write through, and until this change
  nothing asserted they existed.
