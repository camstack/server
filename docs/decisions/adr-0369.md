## Context

The operator asked for a capability that says how a device reaches the
network and, on wifi, how strong its signal is — "same structure as
battery". The obvious name, `connectivity`, was taken: that cap is an
UPSTREAM system's binary view of whether an entity is reachable (a Home
Assistant `binary_sensor` with `device_class: connectivity`), typed for
`Sensor` devices and mapped by the HA provider. Folding a medium and a signal
into it would have made one word mean two things — a bridge's opinion of a
remote entity and a camera's own radio — and every consumer of the existing
cap would have had to learn which one it was reading.

## Decision

A new device-native cap, `network-link`, models the device's OWN link:
`type` (`wifi` | `ethernet` | `cellular` | `unknown`), `signalPercent`
(0..100 or `null`), optional `rssiDbm` and `ssid`, `lastUpdated`. `unknown`
is the absence of a read and `null` is "not known or not applicable", both
skipped by consumers (D315) — a wire is never drawn from silence and a
wireless link is never drawn at 0 % before its first reading. Providers
normalise whatever their firmware reports (bars, RSSI, a vendor scale)
through `signalPercentFromBars` / `signalPercentFromRssi`; the raw value
stays in their debug line. One badge (`NetworkLinkBadge`), one Home
Assistant projection (`link` + `signal` diagnostic sensors), one viewer
glyph — all off the one slice. `connectivity` is untouched.

## Consequences

A second provider (the Dreame robot camera is next) implements the same
slice and gets the badge, the HA entities and the viewer glyph for free.
A wired device shows a wire in the admin UI and nothing in the viewer
footer, where only what can change is worth a glyph. Reolink reads the
link post-login and after each battery read — moments the camera is awake
by construction — and never on a timer of its own, so a sleeping battery
camera is never woken for a bar count.
