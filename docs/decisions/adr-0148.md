## Context

An operator reported on 2026-08-14 that camera 104 showed no recordings. The
first triage said `high`/`mid` had stopped finalizing while `low` still worked,
and pointed at that morning's export-store/export-reconcile change (a5ac170b0).
**Both halves of that were wrong, and checking them is what found the real
defect.**

- Every profile was finalizing. At 20:32:59 CEST the newest segment under
  `/recordings/104/high/2026/08/14/18/` was 25 s old, and so were `mid`, `low`,
  and the same three on 101, 102 and 1441.
- a5ac170b0 touches `export-store.ts`, `export-reconcile.ts` and the timelapse
  renderer. Nothing in it is on the live segment path.
- What HAD happened is that the container restarted at 20:28 (ffmpeg pids
  687–1603, `lstart` 20:28:09–20:29:10) for the `@camstack/server@1.2.110`
  train, which respawned the recorder. The operator's "it started working
  again" and the first triage were describing the same restart from either
  side of it.

The real defect was underneath, and it is not new. `.rec-tmp` held **179 GB**:
102 at 77 GB, 104 at 49 GB, 101 at 48 GB, on an array at 90 % with 204 GB free.
The proof of what it is:

```
/recordings/.rec-tmp/104/high/live.m3u8  → 50 entries, first 1786732111.m4s
                                            (= this writer's 20:28:31 start)
/recordings/.rec-tmp/104/high/*.m4s      → 5 865 files, 5 863 of them named
                                            BEFORE that start, oldest 2026-07-04
```

ffmpeg opens `-segment_list` in truncate mode when it spawns. The playlist is
the segment watcher's only record of what it still owes, so every writer
respawn deletes the claim on anything not yet relocated. Nothing else can find
those files afterwards: staging is flat and dot-prefixed precisely to keep it
out of the hydrate walk, `parseSegmentPath` cannot read it, and the one
`.rec-tmp` sweep that exists (`sweepUnassignedStaging`) only touches locations
with ZERO placement assignments — never the live one. With 2 173 `SegmentWriter
restarting` and 72 `giving up` lines on 2026-08-14 alone, the backlog is a drip
that never drains: 20–100 files a day per profile since 2026-07-04, plus a
3 240-file spike on 2026-07-31.

`segment-watcher.ts` asserted the opposite in a doc comment — that the playlist
"is not cleared between runs", and that statting before classifying "is real
crash recovery". The first is false and the second therefore never fires. A
leftover that describes the right design reads as verification, and this one
covered six weeks of silent loss.

## Decision

Staged media is recovered by **walking the staging directory**, never by
trusting a playlist to have survived the process that wrote it. The recorder
runs one reconcile at boot, over every location, every camera and every profile
— attached or not — and the cutoff is the **boot instant**: a segment named
after it belongs to a live writer and is out of scope, which is what makes the
pass safe to run while recording. Recovery goes through the same
`SegmentStore.onFinalized` as the live path, so a recovered segment is
indistinguishable from one that was never orphaned. **It never deletes**: a
segment that cannot be placed stays exactly where it is for the next boot, and
a duration that cannot be derived from the successor's start falls back to the
nominal segment length rather than claiming the gap.

This pass belongs at BOOT, not at attach. Attach only ever reaches cameras that
are currently recording — device 1438 had a staging backlog while attached to
nothing — and it puts unbounded filesystem work on the path whose whole job is
to start recording now.

## Consequences

- The first recorder boot after this ships relocates the whole backlog. That is
  ~90 000 renames over shfs; the pass yields every 100 files and abandons itself
  within one segment of shutdown, so it cannot starve the write path or blow the
  runner's 5 s SIGTERM grace.
- Recovered footage becomes ordinary indexed footage, so retention sees it for
  the first time. Most of the 179 GB predates the retention window and will be
  evicted — the disk is reclaimed and only the in-window footage becomes
  visible. Both outcomes are wanted; neither is a deletion this pass performs.
- The events-band keep/discard gate is deliberately NOT re-applied to recovered
  segments. It tests against the in-memory trigger map, which a just-booted
  runner has none of, so re-deciding would unlink every recovered segment of an
  `events`-band camera. Keeping a few segments policy would have dropped is the
  strictly safer error.
- The duration of the last orphan of a run, and of any orphan across a run
  boundary, is nominal rather than measured. Timeline coverage under-reports by
  at most one segment per boundary; the alternative — trusting a gap that can be
  hours wide — paints a solid bar over footage that does not exist.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-pipeline/src/recorder/addon/__tests__/staging-reconcile.spec.ts`
— 18 tests. Each gate was verified by neutralising it and confirming the suite
goes red: the previous-run cutoff, the run-boundary duration clamp, the
live-playlist exclusion, per-segment failure isolation, the numeric-device
guard, the profile filter, the per-device `tags: { deviceId }`, and the
shutdown abort with its `remaining` count. No guard script — review only for
the "never trust a playlist to outlive its writer" half of the rule.
