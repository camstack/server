## Context

A group of subjects arriving together produces one track per subject, so one
arrival became several events and several notifications. The answer that had
shipped was a blind cooldown, and the measurement
([docs/design/track-grouping-analysis.md](../design/track-grouping-analysis.md),
live hub 2026-08-14) says it is lossy in both directions at once.

Device 101, rule *Persona su Uscio*, six days:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| distinct tracks that qualified | 116 |
| notifications delivered | 74 |
| qualifying tracks suppressed by the cooldown | **44 (37.9%)** |
| …of which temporally overlap a track that DID fire | **23** |
| …of which carried a confirmed identity | **7** |

In the 2026-08-08 burst *Alice* was recognised at 17:32:38 and never
notified, because *Bob*'s track had fired ten seconds earlier and spent the
window. And the cooldown does not even collapse the burst: a real arrival spans
1–4 minutes against a 60 s window, so the operator still receives 2–3
notifications. They get the VOLUME of a group and the INFORMATION of a single
subject.

Concurrency is the normal case, not the exception: over 48 h across the fleet,
**43 of 120 tracks (36%)** belonged to a cluster of ≥2 temporally overlapping
tracks on one camera. The largest was seven `person` tracks on device 104 over
110 s, three of them identified.

Two designs were considered and one was tried first on paper and rejected. A
DEBOUNCE — hold the first subject, collect for a window, send once — is simpler
and was the obvious shape, but it delays every alert by the window. On an
intrusion rule that is a real cost, and it forces the feature to be an opt-in
nobody enables on the rules that matter most. The reference detector has shipped the other
shape since 0.14.0 (`ReviewSegment`): notify at OPEN, re-notify only when the
object set GROWS, close on an idle cutoff. It costs no latency, so it can be
the default.

## Decision

A rule may declare `groupIdleSec`. While it is set, matching subjects on one
camera are collected into a BURST — one per `(ruleId, deviceId)` — and:

- the FIRST subject enqueues immediately, exactly as before: no added latency;
- a real GROWTH (a new subject, or a name confirmed on a subject already in the
  group) enqueues a replacement row naming everybody, carrying the group's own
  push `tag` so the phone replaces rather than stacks;
- a subject already in the group with nothing new to say enqueues nothing;
- the group closes on an IDLE CUTOFF after the last matching subject — never a
  fixed window;
- the COOLDOWN becomes a budget over groups. It gates the OPEN and never a
  growth, because a growth is not a new notification, it is the same one with
  more in it.

The group is keyed per RULE. A rule that covers several classes gets a mixed
group ("2 persone e 1 animale") because that composition is what the rule asked
for; two different rules never merge, so the car that drives past is its own
notification and is never folded into the people at the door.

`groupIdleSec` absent or `0` is OFF, and OFF is the previous behaviour byte for
byte — including that a group of ONE stamps nothing on the row, so its sentence,
its picture and its push tag are identical to an ungrouped rule's.

## Consequences

- **The notification text CHANGES under the operator.** *"Persona su Front door"*
  becomes *"Bob, Dave e 1 altra persona su Front door"*. [Before: "Person on Entrance"; after: "Bob, Dave and 1 other person on Entrance".] This is the
  behaviour the operator accepted in exchange for zero latency; it is not a
  thing to discover, and the rule editor must say it.
- **A burst produces more OUTBOX ROWS than notifications.** One per revision, by
  design: the durable row is the delivery record and the phone does the
  collapsing via `tag`. The dedup ref carries `:v<n>` (following the existing
  `<trackId>:enhanced` escape) or the outbox's exactly-once guarantee would
  swallow revision 2 as a duplicate of revision 1.
- **Membership is a RAM buffer, not an entity.** Losing it (a respawn) costs the
  open burst its updates and degrades to the previous behaviour. The durable
  half is an audit row (`notification-center:track-groups`) for the timeline
  card, ring-bounded per camera — never age-keyed (D61).
- **Interaction with `waitForEnhancement` is compositional and the order
  matters.** That flag defers the rule to TRACK CLOSE, so with both set the
  group opens on the first member to CLOSE, already named, and grows as later
  members close: later, and complete. With grouping alone the group opens on the
  first object event and picks names up through the growth path. Neither
  combination fires twice for one subject.
- **`runRuleActions` is untouched.** It already fires on the first matching
  subject BEFORE the cooldown, and it must keep doing so: "how often may this
  gate physically move" is the sequence runner's `minDelaySec`, not a
  notification question. An actuation is never grouped and never debounced.
- **Occupancy does not change.** Three people in a group are three people in the
  zone. A group is a relation over subjects; it is never a subject.
- **A group frame is a DELIVERY artefact.** The picture is composed at send from
  the members' normalized bboxes onto the anchor's stored frame, and returned as
  bytes. It is never written into the media store as a track-owned kind — a
  boxed frame is permanently disqualified as model input
  (`retrain-frame-selection.ts`) and a burnt-in box cannot be un-drawn.
- **The geometry is NOT part of the merge.** Grouping is temporal only. The
  cohort test (proximity + motion agreement, calibrated at `d ≤ 1.5`,
  `cos > 0.6` from the measured distribution) is an upgrade that belongs to the
  Phase-2 group ENTITY, not to this delivery merge — the reference detector ships the temporal
  rule alone, and a wrong merge is worse than no merge.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-post-analysis/src/notification-center/__tests__/track-grouping.spec.ts` <!-- drift-ok: deleted with the D143 path, D692 task 17 -->
runs the whole feature on the real centre, the real persist hooks and the real
outbox — the two failure modes (a revision the outbox swallows as a duplicate, a
growth the cooldown eats) are both invisible to a unit test of the buffer. Its
neutralisation is explicit: the same burst with `groupIdleSec: 0` reproduces the
measured defect, so no assertion below it can be passing because of the cooldown.

`group/__tests__/nc-group-buffer.spec.ts` pins the state machine (per-rule
isolation, growth vs unchanged, the idle cutoff, the size bound);
`group/__tests__/nc-group-text.spec.ts` pins that a group of ONE renders exactly
what `ncSubjectText` renders today; `group/__tests__/nc-group-frame.spec.ts`
pins that a stale box is withheld and that nothing composed ever fails a send.

No guard: "is this merge correct" needs ground truth, and no script has it.
