## Context

`pairRiders` (D34, 2026-07-31) folds a person riding a two-wheeler into the
machine: the person detection is dropped, the vehicle survives, and
`Track.hasRider` records that a rider was folded. The fold's own comment says
"a person ON a two-wheeler is ONE subject" — but the box that survived it was
the MACHINE's alone.

Everything that cuts pixels for a track reads `TrackedDetectionOut.bbox`: the
best-shot `thumbnail` crop, `wideCentralSquareLayout`'s central square, the
close-time keyFrame→thumbnail derive, `deriveDetailCropRect`'s subject input.
A machine-only box therefore produced a rider-less keyframe **by
construction** — not a ranking accident, not a capture miss.

Measured on the reference cyclist that `rider-fold-record.spec.ts` already
pins (device 102, 1280×720): person `1122,204 70×164`, bike `1112,271 79×139`.
The emitted subject box was `1112,271 79×139` — the rider's head and torso,
`y 204..271`, sat entirely outside it. Operator, device 102, tracks
`7f50622b` and `9cbc4791`, three minutes apart: *"motocicli e veicoli a due
ruote devono includere il conducente nel keyframe"*.

The fix that was NOT taken: widening the rectangle at the crop site. That is a
second place where a subject rectangle is derived, which is exactly what
[D52](adr-0052.md) forbids, and it would have left the tracker, the zone
membership, the envelope and the importance score still describing a subject
half the size of the one on screen.

## Decision

When a rider is folded, the surviving detection's bbox is the **union** of the
rider's box and the machine's, computed by `riderSubjectBox` in
`rider-pairing.ts` and applied in `frame-processor.ts` **before the tracker**.
It is a strict union — never a pad — so the fold widens the subject and never
moves it. There is exactly one union site; no crop path derives a second
rectangle, and `deriveDetailCropRect` stays the only detail-crop derivation
(D52 intact) — it simply now receives a subject box that already holds the
rider.

Because this file carries frame-local facts across the tracker by **bbox-object
reference** (`labelsByBbox`, `embeddingByBbox`, `maskByBbox`, `plateByBbox`,
`sourceIdByBbox`, `firstLevelBboxById`, the three face tables), the widened box
must INHERIT the machine's entries in every one of them. That transfer lives in
one exhaustive local function, `adoptSubjectBox`, next to the fold.

## Consequences

- The tracker associates, and the stationary gate and the per-class bbox-area
  spawn floor measure, the rider+machine box. For a two-wheeler this is the
  correct subject; for everything else nothing changes, because the fold only
  fires on `bicycle` / `motorcycle` / `motorbike`.
- The union box is taller than the machine's, so `wideCentralSquareLayout`'s
  central square (side = the square that contains the subject) grows with it.
  That is the containment contract doing its job; `MAX_LETTERBOX_FRACTION` is
  untouched and no new padding policy is introduced.
- Adding a new bbox-object-keyed side table to `frame-processor.ts` and not
  adding it to `adoptSubjectBox` silently strips folded riders of that fact.
  The transfer is one function for exactly this reason.

## Enforcement

`pipeline/__tests__/rider-pairing.spec.ts` pins the union geometry against the
measured cyclist; `pipeline/__tests__/rider-fold-record.spec.ts` pins the
EMITTED track bbox (the artefact the crop reads) and that the widened box still
carries the machine's `sourceDetectionId` and embedding. Neutralising the union
turns both red. There is no guard for a forgotten side table beyond that test —
review only.
