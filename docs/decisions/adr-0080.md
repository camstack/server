## Context

HomeKit Secure Video needs a live fragmented-MP4 source, and **CamStack had
none** — the one load-bearing gap in
[the 2026-08-07 assessment](../design/2026-08-07-alexa-events-and-hksv-assessment.md).
The three candidates each failed for a specific reason: the recorder's segments
are ~13 s behind live and carry `libopus` where HKSV takes only AAC; the clip
ring returns one base64 blob and is `-an`; the egress primitive
([D67](./adr-0067.md)) had no mp4 sink at all.

Three choices had to be made, and the obvious answer was wrong in two of them.

**The fragment length.** The natural flag is `-frag_duration`: ask for 4 s
fragments, get 4 s fragments. It cuts at a wall-clock boundary *whether or not a
key frame has arrived*, so a source whose IDR is late produces a fragment that
opens on a delta frame. The muxer accepts it, an argv test passes, and the
consumer rejects a clip nothing in the log explains.

**The consumer interface.** The obvious shape is a callback, because HKSV is the
only consumer being built. The operator's requirement for this item was
explicitly the opposite (roadmap 4b): the live source must be **dual-use**, so a
HomeKit-triggered recording also lands in CamStack as a videoclip source. One
fragmenter, two consumers — a callback makes the second consumer a second ffmpeg
child per camera.

**What a slow consumer costs.** `AudioChunkPlane` drops its oldest chunk on
overflow, and for audio that is a click. Copying that policy here was tried and
is wrong: an fMP4 stream with a hole is not a shorter clip, it is a corrupt one.
`moof` sequence numbers jump, the demuxer desynchronises far from the drop, and
nothing anywhere names the cause.

## Decision

**A fragment boundary is a key frame.** The fMP4 sink emits
`-movflags +frag_keyframe+empty_moov+default_base_moof` and expresses the
negotiated length as `-min_frag_duration` — a flag that can only DELAY a cut,
never move it off a sync sample. `-frag_duration` is forbidden on this sink.
Because the cadence is then the key-frame cadence, a caller that wants
N-second fragments must also pin `FfmpegVideoEncodePlan.forceKeyFramesSeconds`;
on a smart-COPY branch it cannot, so `fragmentMs` degrades from a target to a
floor and the handle reports `fragmentLengthEnforced: false`.

**The fragments reach consumers on a subscribable plane, never a callback.**
`Fmp4FragmentPlane` fans one splitter's units to N independent subscriptions,
retains the initialisation segment so a subscriber attaching mid-stream can
decode what follows, and computes `isLast` at delivery time. **A subscription
that falls behind is CLOSED with a reason; it is never gapped.**

> **Amended by [D84](./adr-0084.md), 2026-08-07.** Two things here did not
> survive contact with a consumer. The three files listed above **moved into
> `@camstack/types`**: this record says the fragment consumers are "in-process"
> and hold a live plane object, and the first real one — the HKSV delegate in
> `addon-export-hap` — is a `hub-only` addon in its own runner, so it never
> could. And `-min_frag_duration` is now emitted at 90 % of `fragmentMs`, not at
> `fragmentMs`: equal to the source's key-frame grid it loses the race at the
> boundary and DOUBLES every fragment (measured 7944 ms for a negotiated 4000).

## Consequences

- **The fragment length is a property of the ENCODER, not of the muxer.** A
  request that reaches the copy branch gets source-GOP fragments. That is
  reported twice — in the handle and at `warn` with `tags: { deviceId }` — and
  it is a real limitation a phase-4 caller has to decide about, not a bug.
- **A slow consumer loses its whole stream, deliberately.** For HKSV that means
  iOS gets a truncated clip instead of a corrupt one; the delegate must handle a
  subscription that ends early rather than assume it completed.
- **Audio on this transport is AAC and is not negotiable.** HKSV accepts AAC-LC
  and AAC-ELD, and an fMP4 fragment has no second plane to graft an SDP onto.
  This is why the recorder — deliberately `libopus`, for the WebRTC copy path —
  can never serve HKSV whatever else changes.
- **The transport is in the sharing key**, alongside the fragment length. Two
  consumers that negotiated different lengths cannot share a child.
- **The cap surface is deferred.** `fragments` is unreachable from
  `EgressTranscodeRequest`: its consumers are in-process and hold a live object,
  and a `fragmentMs` on the schema would be a codegen run plus a
  `@camstack/system` train for a field nothing off-node can call.
  `egressTransportFromRequest` is the single place that learns to read it when
  one of them lands off-node.

## Enforcement

`packages/types/src/__tests__/ffmpeg-invocation.spec.ts` asserts the three
movflags individually, the microsecond conversion, and that `-frag_duration`
never appears. `packages/types/src/__tests__/fmp4-box-splitter.spec.ts`
runs the argv the builder produces through a REAL ffmpeg and asserts that a
longer negotiated length yields fewer fragments **over a fixed key-frame grid** —
written the obvious way (grid tied to the length) that test stays green with
`-min_frag_duration` deleted, because the grid alone explains the difference.
`fmp4-fragment-plane.spec.ts` asserts the close-not-gap policy and that exactly
one packet carries `isLast`. Every one of these was verified by deleting the fix
and watching it go red.
