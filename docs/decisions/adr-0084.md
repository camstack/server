## Context

[D80](./adr-0080.md) built a live fMP4 source and deliberately wired nothing to
it. Phases 3 and 4 — the prebuffer, then the delegate and the advertisement —
were gated on one measurement the assessment refused to guess at (§A4.2): a
HomeKit Secure Video prebuffer means an ffmpeg that runs whether or not anyone is
watching, and [D67](./adr-0067.md) had priced a 4K→1080p transcode at ~77 % of a
core on a hub already sitting at 70-95 %.

Three things were found, and **two of them contradict what D80 assumed**.

**D80's premise about who consumes the plane was wrong.** It records the
consumers as "in-process", so the transport lives in `addon-pipeline`'s
stream-broker and is reached through `EgressTranscodeManager.acquireFragments`.
`addon-export-hap` is `placement: hub-only` in its own runner ([D2](./adr-0002.md)),
so the HKSV delegate cannot hold that plane object at all. There was no cap to
add — a live plane cannot cross the boundary in any shape — so the choice was a
second splitter in the exporter or one copy where the bytes are made.

**The measurement.** Camera 101's live restream, the exact argv
`buildFmp4SinkArgs` emits, 45-second samples, `ps` per second:

| branch | CPU (1 core = 100 %) | RSS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-c:v copy` 1280×720, `-an` | 0.3 % | 27 MB |
| `-c:v copy` 1280×720 + AAC | **0.7 %** | 30 MB |
| `-c:v copy` 3840×2160 + AAC | **2.8 %** | 77 MB |
| 4K→1080p `libx264 veryfast` + AAC | **89.9 %** | 577 MB |

**128×.** The transcode figure confirms D67; the copy figure is what makes a
permanent per-camera prebuffer schedulable at all.

**And a live bug in the shipped-but-inert phase-1 sink.** Measuring the fragment
cadence showed 7944 ms where 4000 was negotiated, on both the 720p and the 4K
slot. Not the source GOP: 101's key-frame grid is 4.0 s on both
(`stream-params`: 40 f @ 10 fps, 100 f @ 25 fps). `-min_frag_duration 4000000`
puts the muxer's deadline on the same instant as the key frame, loses the race,
and skips to the next one. Re-run at 3600000 the median gap was 3973 / 3998 ms.
**A key-frame grid equal to the requested fragment length is the commonest
camera configuration there is**, and it doubled every fragment — which HKSV
rejects, because a fragment must not exceed the length the controller selected.

## Decision

**The fMP4 transport is part of the ffmpeg PRIMITIVE.** `fmp4-box-splitter`,
`fmp4-fragment-plane` and `fmp4-fragment-child` move to `@camstack/types`
(`ffmpeg/`, the plane and child under the `/node` subpath) beside the sink whose
bytes they read. `EgressTranscodeManager` keeps only broker business — sharing
key, refcount, release grace.

**`-min_frag_duration` is emitted at 90 % of `fragmentMs`**, never at
`fragmentMs`. `-frag_duration` remains forbidden (D80).

**A permanent prebuffer runs on the COPY branch or not at all.** HKSV takes
H.264, so the rule is enforceable exactly: `pickRecordingSource` refuses a camera
whose enabled slots are all H.265, and prefers the largest slot at or below
1080p — a 4 s fragment is 145 KB at 720p and 3.6 MB at 4K, and the ring holds
fragments in the exporter's heap.

**The prebuffer ring is bounded by TIME and BYTES, measured on ARRIVAL.** The
splitter never parses `tfdt` (D80: a second opinion about a fact the muxer owns),
and a prebuffer cares how long ago bytes turned up. A subscriber that requests
the replay has its queue capacity raised by the replay length — a replay is not
backlog, and closing a subscriber as slow for the prebuffer it asked for would be
guaranteed with a default capacity of 4 and a full ring.

**HKSV is per-camera and OFF by default**, and the delegate and the
advertisement are built by ONE function that returns both or neither
([D50](./adr-0050.md)). Every refusal is logged with a reason and
`tags: { deviceId }`.

## Consequences

- **This is a framework change**: `@camstack/types` now carries the transport, so
  it ships on the `@camstack/server` train, and `addon-pipeline` /
  `addon-export-hap` must be deployed against a hub that already has it.
- **A camera with an H.265-only stream shows no recording toggle**, permanently,
  by design. The fix is to give it an H.264 slot, never to add a transcode.
- **A camera whose key-frame interval exceeds 8 s shows no toggle either.** We
  advertise the shortest offered fragment length that covers the source GOP, and
  offer only 4000 and 8000 ms.
- **When `stream-params` is not bound we GUESS 4000 ms.** The delegate measures
  the arriving cadence and warns at `warn` with the deviceId when it is wrong —
  which is the only symptom, because iOS discards an over-long fragment silently.
- **The prebuffer dies with its child.** A respawn is bounded and backed off
  (D6), and the gap is logged: a source that silently stopped filling reads,
  from the delegate, exactly like a camera nothing happens on.
- **`sensors.motion` moves onto the controller** when recording is on. HKSV
  derives its MOTION trigger from the controller's own service and is blind to
  any other, and this is a STRUCTURAL accessory change (`c#`) — so flipping the
  setting rebuilds the accessory, and iOS may need the camera re-added.

## Enforcement

`packages/types/src/__tests__/ffmpeg-invocation.spec.ts` asserts the emitted
`-min_frag_duration` is strictly below `fragmentMs` and within 15 % of it, over
three lengths. `fmp4-fragment-plane.spec.ts` covers both ring bounds, the
never-evict-the-newest rule, the subscribe-time window, the init-clears-the-ring
rule and the capacity raise. `addon-export-hap/src/hksv/__tests__/` covers the
picker's refusals, the advertisement's derivation, and the delegate's contract —
driven against a REAL `Fmp4FragmentPlane`, including the two ways `isLast` can be
lost. `camera-accessory-recording.spec.ts` asserts both halves of the
advertisement: absent by default, both services present when the operator turns
it on, and absent again for an H.265 camera even when they do.

The capacity raise, the subscribe-time trim, the delegate's refusal and its
end-of-stream marker were each verified by DELETING the fix and watching the
matching test go red.

## Correction — 2026-08-08, the first real exercise

The advertisement above was built and never negotiated. On its first day it
never was: camera 101 sat with `Active = true` and **no
`SelectedCameraRecordingConfiguration` at all** for fourteen hours, so the
prebuffer never started and none of the runtime this record describes was ever
reached.

**The advertised `Resolution` may not carry the PROBED frame rate.** It carried
`fpsByProfile`, so 101 offered `[1280, 720, 10]` — the controller has to find an
acceptable configuration in that list before it writes one back, and it did not.
Quantising onto `{15, 24, 30}` (`normaliseAdvertisedFps`) produced
`iOS selected a recording configuration` **two seconds** after the deploy.

The second reason is the one that would have bitten later anyway.
`RecordingManagement` hashes the supported-configuration TLVs and `deserialize`
DISCARDS the controller's stored selection when the hash moves — after which
every HDS `DATA_SEND OPEN` is answered `INVALID_CONFIGURATION`. The advertised
resolution was the one hashed input that came from a measurement, and camera 104
probed 9 fps then 10 across two restarts. A quantised rate gives the probe a wide
band to drift inside without the advertisement changing at all, and the selection
now survives a restart (verified: `hasConfiguration=true` on the next boot).

**`longestFragmentGapMs` was measuring the wrong interval.** It seeded from the
SUBSCRIBE time, so the fragment child's spawn, RTSP connect and wait-for-key-frame
were charged to the first fragment: 101's first stream reported 6073 ms against a
source cutting a metronomic 4 s, and the remedy this record prescribes for that
warning is a change to the CAMERA's key-frame grid. **A detector whose false
positive invites an irreversible hardware change is worse than no detector.**
Gaps are now measured between consecutive fragments only — the same camera then
reported 3993 — and subscribe→first-fragment is its own number,
`msToFirstFragmentMs`.

**Two writes, two controllers, and one of them was invisible.** The iPhone writes
`Active`; the resident home hub writes the configuration and opens the stream.
Both are `adminOnlyAccess: [WRITE]`, and hap-nodejs refuses a non-admin with
`INSUFFICIENT_PRIVILEGES` emitting no characteristic warning and nothing above
`debug` — so "the hub cannot" and "the hub did not" were the same silence for the
whole investigation. `describeHapControllers` censuses the open connections and
their permissions onto the recording-active line, and warns when recording is on,
no configuration exists and no connected controller may write one.

Motion is the trigger, and it was pushed to HomeKit with no log line at all.
It has one now, with `tags: { deviceId }` and whether the service is the
HKSV-adopted one.
