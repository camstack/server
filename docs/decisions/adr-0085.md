> **Superseded by [D91](./adr-0091.md) the same day, on the operator's report of
> the very consequence this record states.** The on-demand seam produced the
> best-looking mp4 and the WRONG six seconds: `eventToDialMs=7054` meant the clip
> covered [event+7 s, event+13 s] while the gif beside it, cut from the ring,
> covered [event−3 s, event+5 s]. The seam and its assembler are deleted; event
> media is now produced once, by the broker, out of the prebuffer it already
> holds. Read this record for what it MEASURED — the dial, the absence of a
> pre-roll, the truncation contract — not for what it decided.

## Context

[D80](./adr-0080.md) built the fragment seam for HomeKit Secure Video and named
a second consumer it deliberately did not build: the operator's requirement for
[roadmap 4b](../roadmap.md) was that the live fMP4 source be **dual-use**, so a
notification clip stops being a re-encode. [D84](./adr-0084.md) then landed the
first consumer and moved the transport into `@camstack/types` when D80's premise
about who could hold a plane turned out to be wrong.

What the notification centre produces today is `streamBroker.renderPreBufferClip`
([D32](./adr-0032.md)): the broker's encoded ring, re-encoded through `libx264
-crf 28` at **480 px wide, 5 fps, 2× speed, `-an`**. It is a thumbnail with
motion. The seam can hand over the camera's own H.264 at its own resolution,
real time, with in-band AAC, for the cost of a copy.

Three things had to be decided, and the first guess was wrong in two of them.

**Where the consumer lives.** The obvious answer is a new
`streamBroker.renderFragmentClip` cap method, assembling in the process where
the bytes are made. That is a `*.cap.ts` change, a codegen run and a
`@camstack/system` train — to move bytes that are already reachable over RTSP.
And D84 had already settled the shape: a plane is a live object, no capability
can carry one, so the HKSV delegate spawns its own child against the broker's
restream from its own runner. The notification centre is in a third runner and
can do exactly the same.

**Whether the transport is on the framework train.** `@camstack/types/node` is a
subpath and the framework contract says every subpath is host-resolved
([D15](./adr-0015.md)) — which would make this feature wait for a published
`@camstack/server`. It does not: `addon-post-analysis` builds `self-contained`,
so `@camstack/types` and all its subpaths are **inlined into the addon bundle**.
Verified in the dist, not inferred from the preset's comments.

**Whether an on-demand child gets any pre-roll.** `RtspRestreamer` bursts the
current GOP to a joining session, so it looked as though the first fragment
would open before we dialed. Measured against 101's 720p slot with the exact
argv, the real child and the real assembler:

| dial → init | fragments | media | wall clock | bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4479 ms | 3 × ~141 KB | 12.03 s | 12.42 s | 425 KB (`ffprobe`: 1280×720 h264 + aac, decodes clean) |

Media and wall clock differ by **387 ms — the dial**. Nothing from before the
spawn survives: ffmpeg discards up to the first key frame it can open on. There
is no pre-roll, and the same fact sets the floor on the start timeout.

## Decision

**A notification clip is cut from the seam ON DEMAND, in `addon-post-analysis`,
and it introduces NO permanent session.** The child is spawned at the event and
killed when the clip is assembled. A camera that already has a warm plane
(HKSV's) is not reached for — a plane cannot cross a runner boundary, and adding
a per-camera warm child is a new permanent cost, therefore an operator decision
and not an implementation one.

**The consequence is stated, not hidden: `preRollSec` is not honoured on this
path.** A rule that needs the approach rather than the aftermath is served by
the clip ring, which retains the past and always will.

**Every decline falls back to the clip ring and is LOGGED in one place**, with a
code, a sentence and `tags: { deviceId }` — `Fmp4ClipService.renderClip`, never
the call site. Two cameras silently attaching two different kinds of clip is
exactly what an operator cannot diagnose from a phone.

**The seam copies or it declines.** An H.265-only camera is refused, because a
transcode is the old path with extra steps at 128× the cost (D84's measurement).
The slot is the largest H.264 one at or below 1080p.

**A truncated clip is returned MARKED, and two files are never written at all**:
one with no initialisation segment, and one with an init and no media. Any
contiguous prefix of `init` + fragments is a playable mp4, so truncation is
honest; an init alone is a file every player opens and nothing plays.

## Consequences

- **Nothing ships on the framework train for this.** `camstack deploy
  packages/addon-post-analysis` is the whole deployment, and it works against a
  hub whose `@camstack/types` predates the transport.
- **A clip now costs an ffmpeg spawn per notification that asks for one**, for
  as long as the window plus one key-frame interval. Bounded at
  `CLIP_TOTAL_BUDGET_MS` (25 s) and, on the copy branch, at the 0.7 %-of-a-core
  figure D84 measured — for seconds, not permanently.
- **A camera the seam cannot start on is cooled down for ten minutes**, not
  retried on every notification forever. The recovery is logged with the number
  of attempts it swallowed, because a silent recovery is as bad as a silent
  failure.
- **The byte cap is 5 MiB and it is the DEGRADE ENGINE's number**, not a
  guess: an over-`maxBytes` attachment is dropped outright rather than degraded
  to a link, and pushover's 5 MiB is the smallest cap declared. A clip truncated
  to fit and delivered beats a whole one thrown away.
- **A GIF never touches this path.** A GIF is a re-encode by definition.
- **This does not remove the clip ring**, and must not: it is the fallback for
  H.265, for a broker on another node, for a camera mid-reconnect, and it is the
  only path with a pre-roll.

## Enforcement

The tests named here were deleted with the code (see D91); they covered the picker's three refusals and its ceiling, the assembler (both files it
refuses to write, the window close, each truncation reason, and that the
subscription is released on every path) driven against a **real**
`Fmp4FragmentPlane`, and the service's decline paths — including that all four
of them emit exactly one line carrying the code, the reason and the deviceId.

Every gate was verified by DELETING it and watching the matching test go red:
the `no-init` refusal, the `no-fragments` refusal, the truncation mark, the
window close, the cooldown, the child stop, the H.265 refusal and the decline
log. The end-to-end proof is the measurement above — a real camera, a real
child, `ffprobe` on the bytes — not a fixture.
