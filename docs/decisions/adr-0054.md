## Context

The instrumentation shipped as R4 ([D53](adr-0053.md)) finally read the
controller's own Receiver Reports. One 19.27 s session on `101/mid`, live on
2026-08-06:

```
negotiatedMaxBitrateKbps=299  mtu=1378  transcode=false
videoPacketsForwarded=737     videoReceiverReports={reportsParsed:62,
  unreadable:0, worstFractionLostPct:51.2, lastCumulativePacketsLost:450,
  maxJitter:272324}
videoLossVerdict=transmit-suspect
```

737 packets × 1378 B over 19.27 s ≈ **421 kbps against a negotiated 299**, and
iOS reported losing **450 of the 737**. Every prior round — video-leg Sender
Reports (R1), an honest frame rate (R2), withdrawing the HKSV stub (R3) — had
addressed *what* we send. None of them concerned **how much**, and under
`-c:v copy` there was no lever to pull even in principle.

Two more measurements, taken while designing the fix, changed its shape:

1. **The obvious data source is a trap.** `webrtcSession.listStreams` reports a
   per-slot `bitrateKbps`, and it is a *measured flow* — meaningless for a slot
   nobody is consuming. Live, it reported `mid = 9 kbps` for the very slot that
   had just delivered ~421 kbps, `low = 5`, `high = 5441`. Selecting on that
   reading admits every slot and changes nothing.
2. **The overshoot and the burst are one phenomenon.** `ffprobe` over 200 frames
   of `101/mid`: 5 key frames averaging **224 287 B**, 195 P-frames averaging
   **103 B** — **98.2 % of the bytes are key frames**. A 224 KB IDR at
   `mtu = 1378` is ~163 RTP packets emitted back to back inside one 100 ms frame
   slot. 5 IDRs per 20 s is 448 kbps, which *is* the 421 kbps measured on the
   wire. So the 3.03 s peak jitter and the loss concentrated at the head of the
   session are the same defect as the volume, not a second one.

The alternative considered and rejected was **transcode unconditionally**: it is
simple and always honours the cap, but it puts an x264 encoder on a hub already
measured at 70–95 % main-thread with load average 29, for cameras whose
substream may already fit. The other rejected alternative was **select on the
measured flow rate**, which the numbers above disqualify outright.

## Decision

An exporter must not put more on the wire than the consumer negotiated.
Concretely, for every session:

- **Pass-through is conditional.** A slot is copied only when its rate is
  *known* to fit the negotiated ceiling, less a headroom factor covering
  packetisation. The authority is the camera's **configured** encoder bitrate
  (`streamParams.getStatus` → `main`/`sub`/`ext`, mapped onto the profile slot
  through the assigned cam-stream's resolution and frame rate), because that is
  the envelope; a measured flow rate is a **lower bound** and may therefore only
  ever prove a slot does *not* fit, never that it does.
- **An unknown rate is not a fit.** Absent, ambiguous or implausible evidence
  means transcode. Guessing a number here silently re-creates the overshoot.
- **Otherwise transcode, with a real cap**: `-b:v` / `-maxrate` and a
  **one-second** `-bufsize` at the budget, `-r` at a rate the source can
  actually produce, `-profile:v baseline -level 3.1 -tune zerolatency`.
- **The selection narrows the candidate set; it never becomes a second opinion
  about which slot serves which resolution.** The same `pickPreferredRtspEntry`
  D51 derives the advertisement from still decides, over the affordable subset.
- **A pinned `streamPreference` outranks the budget.** The pin is an operator
  instruction about which slot to serve; the budget is a consumer constraint
  about how much of it to send. An over-budget pinned slot is transcoded, never
  silently swapped for a cheaper one. Only `auto` lets the budget change which
  slot wins.

## Consequences

- **On the current fleet, HomeKit transcodes.** Both exposed cameras configure
  every slot at ≥ 1024 kbps while iOS negotiated 299, so nothing fits. Measured
  cost of one such session, `-preset ultrafast`, on an Apple-silicon core:
  **720p@10 → 1.56 s CPU per 20.6 s wall, i.e. ~7.6 % of one core** (pass-through
  is 0.9 %). The expensive shape is a 1080p ask, which the picker resolves onto
  the 4 K slot: **4K→1080p@25 costs 15.3 s CPU per 19.9 s wall, ~77 % of one
  core**, and it is the *decode* that dominates. If HomeKit usage grows beyond a
  couple of concurrent sessions, hardware-accelerated decode is the lever — not
  a different selection rule.
- **The cap also fixes the burst**, which is why no separate pacer was added: at
  269 kbps the encoder's I-frame measured **22 328 B** against the source's
  **224 287 B**, a 10× smaller burst, and the VBV window is what forces it.
- **A camera-side fix outranks this one.** A slot that is 98 % key-frame bytes is
  a GOP/bitrate misconfiguration; correcting it would let pass-through win and
  cost the hub nothing.
- **The advertisement (D51) is unchanged and stays derivable.** When nothing
  fits, the transcode dials the slot the *unfiltered* picker chooses — the same
  slot the advertisement was derived from — and encodes at that slot's advertised
  rate, so the claim stays true by construction. The residual: when a *different*
  slot fits and is copied, the delivered rate can differ from the rate advertised
  for the negotiated resolution. That case logs
  `negotiated fps does NOT match the rate we are about to deliver`; it is not
  silently absorbed.
- **Two extra cap reads per session start** (`getCameraStreams`,
  `streamParams.getStatus`). Deliberately not hoisted to accessory-build time
  like the frame-rate probe: an operator who lowers a substream's bitrate should
  get pass-through on the next session, not after the next accessory rebuild.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-export-hap/src/mappers/builders/__tests__/stream-bitrate.spec.ts`
pins the rule against the live 101 numbers: that 299 kbps admits no 101 slot,
that a 9 kbps flow reading is discarded, that a measured rate may only disprove a
fit, that an ambiguous encoder match yields unknown rather than a number, that an
H.265 slot is never passed through, and that the emitted argument vector carries
`-maxrate`/`-bufsize` at the budget. Each was removed in turn and the suite went
red — 7 tests on the fit gate, 1 each on the plausibility floor, the rate cap,
the fps clamp, the codec gate and the headroom.

**What no test can establish:** whether this ends the teardown. 421 vs 299 is
1.4× and does not by itself explain 61 % loss. The pass condition is unchanged
and remains a live observation: one session whose
`export-hap: stream session summary` no longer reads
`videoLossVerdict=transmit-suspect`.
