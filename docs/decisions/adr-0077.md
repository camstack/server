## Context

The operator toggled camera 104's microphone off and back on
([D74](adr-0074.md)) and reported two things: the live view froze until
pause+play, and **audio never came back**. Both were true, both were the same
defect, and neither was visible from any log.

The broker's automatic reconnect is not the problem — it works. A camera-side
encoder restart drops the RTSP connection, `buildRtpStreamCallbacks().onError`
fires, `scheduleReconnect` re-dials with backoff, and the `StreamBroker` object
survives so every subscription (`rtpVideoCallbacks`, the audio-chunk plane, the
frame plane) is still attached. Packets resume.

**The problem is WHEN it reconnects.** The write walks the camera channel by
channel, so the camera is restarting encoders for several seconds, and the
re-dial lands in the middle of that. Measured on 104 (2026-08-07):

```
16:01:46  hik privacy audio: microphone written {enabled=false channels=101,102,103}
16:01:49  (native:main) native RTSP error {error="RTSP connection closed unexpectedly"}
16:01:50  (native:main) Broker starting … Video track {codec=h264}      ← no Audio track
16:01:52  (native:sub)  … same
16:01:56  (native:slot-3) … same
16:01:57  hik privacy audio: microphone written {enabled=true …}
          — and then nothing, for forty minutes.
```

Every broker settled on a video-only session against a camera whose audio mux
came back seconds later. Nothing asked again, and nothing could: the readers
were healthy, `hasDemand()` was satisfied, `scheduleReconnect` had no reason to
fire. `kickReconnect` would not have helped either — it only shortens a backoff
that is already running (`if (!this.reconnectTimer) return`). The only cure
available was disabling the whole device for longer than the 30 s catalog
reconcile so the brokers were released and rebuilt.

The freeze is the same event seen from the consumer side. A re-dial gives the
session a new SSRC and a fresh random sequence/timestamp base, while werift's
sender offset was latched once, on the first packet of the connection that just
died. The client sees a wild sequence jump and holds its last frame. Pause+play
"fixes" it because it tears the session down and builds a new one.

`armSourceSilenceWatchdog` could not have caught any of this: `sourceMediaSeen`
is a latch that never resets, so five seconds after feed start the watchdog is
inert for the session's whole life. It was built for a cold dial that never
delivers and is structurally blind to a mid-session drop.

## Decision

**A source restart is not transparent, and the three layers each own one part
of admitting it.**

1. **The WRITER announces it.** A provider that has just restarted a camera's
   encoder emits `EventCategory.StreamParamsChanged` — which is exactly what it
   means, the shape of what the camera emits changed — rather than relying on
   the broker to notice. Hikvision's and Reolink's `setAudioEnabled` do; so does
   `stream-params.setProfile`, which already did.
2. **The BROKER re-dials after the camera settles.**
   `StreamBrokerManager.redialDeviceSources` fans out to every non-derived
   broker of that device after a settle delay, and `StreamBroker.redial()` tears
   down a HEALTHY reader to do it. Routed through `scheduleReconnect` → `start()`
   so the 2026-07-23 dial-storm rate limit still governs every retry. Derived
   brokers are excluded: they consume a source broker rather than dialing the
   camera.
3. **Every attached CONSUMER re-syncs.** `StreamBroker.onSourceRestart()` fires
   on every dial after the first — at the DIAL, not the first packet, so a
   restart that never delivers is still observable. `AdaptiveSession.onSourceRestarted`
   arms `rtpRebasePending` (which continues the outbound stream from the last
   packet actually SENT — the `videoRtpSynced` branch latches onto the source's
   raw sequence, correct for the first packet ever and a visible jump here),
   clears `rtpBootstrapDone` so the fresh GOP replays instead of waiting a
   keyframe interval, drops the previous stream's repacketizer and SSRC, and
   re-arms the silence watchdog against the new connection.

Two consequences are not optional:

- **The observer is a `Set`, not the single-slot `demandObserver`.** One broker
  carries many WebRTC sessions and each has its own mapping to re-latch.
- **A no-op write announces nothing.** `setAudioEnabled` with every channel
  already in the target state returns without writing and without nudging — a
  no-op that re-dials three RTSP sessions is worse than the no-op it was asked
  to perform.

**And the per-channel writes go in PARALLEL.** Measured on 104: 25.5 s
sequential for three `setStreamingChannel` PUTs; 50–150 ms for the same mutation
when every channel is already in the target state. Essentially all of it was the
camera restarting an encoder per PUT and answering only when done. They address
three different ISAPI resources with no shared read-modify-write, so there is
nothing to serialise — the provider's own line reports `elapsedMs=4299` after
the change. **Reolink stays sequential**, and that is not an oversight: `setEnc`
is a whole-config read-modify-write and concurrent calls would clobber each
other.

Parallel also collapses three staggered restart windows into one, which is what
the re-dial has to wait out.

Hikvision gains the verify-and-revert D74 gave Reolink, in **both** directions.
The enable is the direction that broke 104 in production, and a half-restore
leaves the operator believing sound is back on a stream that is still silent.

## Consequences

- A camera-config write that restarts an encoder now costs one extra re-dial per
  source, ~2 s after the write. That is deliberate: the alternative is the state
  above, which is unrecoverable without operator action.
- `StreamParamsChanged` is LOSSY by contract. Losing it means the broker keeps a
  stale session until something else re-dials — the status quo this repairs, not
  a new regression.
- The transcode leg is **not** covered. Its video arrives via `leg.attachVideo`,
  and a source drop that kills the ffmpeg child leaves `egress-transcode-manager`
  holding a corpse with nothing re-acquiring. Known, tracked, not this record.
- The admin switch group became optimistic-with-rollback in the same change. It
  is not cosmetic: two rows of that group write the CAMERA, and awaiting a
  multi-second device round trip with every button disabled reads as a hung page.

## Enforcement

- `packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/webrtc/__tests__/session-source-restart.spec.ts`
  — the re-sync: the REBASE is armed and the first-packet sync is not, the
  bootstrap replays, the repacketizer and SSRC are dropped, the silence guard
  re-arms, and a closed session does nothing. Verified discriminating by
  removing the two assignments and confirming two failures.
- `packages/addon-provider-hikvision/src/__tests__/hikvision-stream-audio.spec.ts`
  — `classifyAudioWrite`: a channel that took the call and did not move is
  REFUSED, a channel that stopped answering is REFUSED rather than absent, and
  the ENABLE direction is covered explicitly because that is the one that broke
  production.
- Live, on 104 (2026-08-07 16:40): un-mute → `encoder restarted — nudging the
  broker to re-dial` → `re-dialing its source brokers {settleMs=2000}` → three
  `source redial` lines → three `Broker starting` each followed by
  `Audio track {codec=PCMU}` and `audio decoder initialized`. Before the change
  the same sequence produced three video-only sessions and stopped.
