## Context

An operator's report, precise enough to be a diagnosis on its own: entering the
timeline from live, the first seconds of a drag are instant, and **at the first
slow-down the granularity collapses and never comes back** for the rest of the
gesture.

Both halves of that sentence are the same accounting bug.

- A drag OPENS in `jump` mode: with no previous target there is no delta, so
  every push is one access unit, emits one position, and takes one ack. Credit
  recycles per round trip and the drag flies.
- Slowing down puts consecutive targets inside the 4 s sweep window
  ([D68](./adr-0068.md)), so a push becomes a SWEEP of ~25 access units — but it
  is still ONE push and still emits ONE position.
- The feeder charged the push ONE credit ([D41](./adr-0041.md), cap 2) and
  repaid it one FRAME at a time, releasing the credit only when the head of the
  outstanding queue reached zero frames. A viewer without
  `requestVideoFrameCallback` acks once per position — so a 25-frame sweep could
  produce exactly one ack, forever short of the 25 the feeder was waiting for.
  Two sweeps drained the cap and the drag parked permanently: `beginScrub` is a
  documented no-op mid-drag, and nothing timed a park out. Measured in the
  field: parks after 18 pushes with 3 acks in hand.
- The client then made it stick. Its push-depth verdict was one-way for the rest
  of the drag, and its liveness proof was a RENDERED frame — a signal a platform
  without rvfc never emits at all, so the counter only ever went up and the push
  depth was abandoned after three positions on **every** drag there, falling
  back to the 4 s keyframe sweep. That is the "never comes back".

The per-frame settle was not an oversight; it was a deliberate guard against the
opposite failure, and the comment defending it named a real measurement
(`mode=sweep aus=26 creditLeft=1`). With rvfc the viewer acks per rendered
frame, so settling per PUSH would refund one credit per frame and the gate could
never close. **Both readings are right, on different platforms** — which is why
the unit could not be fixed by choosing one of them.

## Decision

The receiver credit bounds PUSHES IN FLIGHT, not frames. A push spends one
credit whatever it puts on the wire, and the FIRST ack naming it repays that
credit. The ack names its push: the `position` message a push emits carries a
`seq`, the viewer echoes the newest `seq` it has seen on `scrubAck`, and the
feeder settles every outstanding push at or before it. Surplus acks (an rvfc
viewer sends one per rendered frame) name an already-settled push and are
no-ops; a `seq` no outstanding push matches — a late ack from a drag that
ended — banks nothing. The sequence is monotonic for the life of the feeder,
never per drag, so a stale ack can never settle a fresh push.

A push that goes unacknowledged for `SCRUB_ACK_TIMEOUT_MS` (2 s) is written off
and its credit returned, logged once per drag and counted on the drag census
(`ackTimeouts`). This closes the residual D41 accepted explicitly: "if every
outstanding frame is lost in RTP and no render ever fires, the drag stalls until
the commit".

Client side: an abandon of the push depth is a judgement about NOW and is lifted
by evidence — and the evidence is the strongest signal the PLATFORM has, a
rendered frame under rvfc, else a delivered position.

## Consequences

- Backpressure is preserved by the CAP, not by the frame count: at most two
  pushes are in flight, so a thin pipe still parks. What changed is that the
  park is now released by one ack instead of N.
- A sweep can put ~25 access units on the wire for one credit. That is the
  intended cost of frame-granular scrub (D68) and is bounded by the coalescing
  window plus the cap — never by the ack unit.
- `seq` is optional on the wire in both directions. A server without it still
  gets a bare ack; a viewer without it gets acks that settle the OLDEST
  outstanding push — the safe direction, which can repay too early but can never
  strand the drag. So the server fix alone restores the operator's granularity;
  the viewer fix makes the rvfc half exact.
- On a platform with no render callback, a push session that delivers but cannot
  DECODE is now indistinguishable from one that paints, and will not be
  abandoned. The alternative there was not a better verdict — it was abandoning
  the push depth on every drag, which is what shipped.

## Enforcement

Tests, each verified to fail with its fix removed
(`packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/recorded/__tests__/scrub-credit.spec.ts`):
a slowed drag that sweeps keeps pushing under one-ack-per-position; the
granularity after the slow-down matches the granularity before it; surplus acks
naming a settled push repay nothing; an ack from a previous drag settles
nothing; a lost ack unparks the drag and is counted. The client half is pinned
in the viewer repo (`embed/src/playback/__tests__/recorded-track-session.spec.ts`,
`embed/src/scrub/__tests__/byte-range-scrub-tier.spec.ts`).
