## Context

With the receiver credit fixed ([D150](./adr-0150.md)) the scrub no longer parks
— and the operator still called it slow, against a direct comparison: the reference NVR
on the SAME camera looks like ≥20 fps where this reads about 3.

The census says why, on one line (hall 104, `low`, 2026-08-14):

```
ticks=17 dragMs=5496 tickMsP50=270 tickMsMax=684
locateMsP50=1 readMsP50=263 readMsMax=681 demuxMsP50=1 ackMsP50=23
gopReads=17 readBytes=2290982 coverMemo=0 coverCache=0 coverMiss=17
jumps=17 jumpsBack=16 sweeps=0 parks=0 ackTimeouts=0
```

**The tick IS the read**: 263 ms of a 270 ms tick, for a cold ~135 KB fragment
off the HDD array through shfs/FUSE. Locate is memoised (1 ms), demux is
in-process (1 ms), the network is not the problem at all (`ackMsP50 = 23`). One
read at a time is therefore a hard ~3.8 push/s ceiling.

**The coverage tiers never fire, and the reason is not a broken cache.**
`coverMemo = 0` is the load-bearing number: the memo is the fragment the
PREVIOUS tick demuxed, so zero hits across 17 consecutive ticks proves the
targets never land in the same ~4 s fragment twice. `jumps = 17` says the same
thing from the other side — a jump is what the feeder chooses when the delta
leaves the 4 s sweep window. The steps are tens of seconds: the timeline maps
~8.8 s per CSS pixel, and a 270 ms tick gives the finger time to travel. There
is no locality to cache, and it feeds itself — the slower the tick, the bigger
the next step, the further the next read.

`jumpsBack = 16` is genuine, not an artefact: `scrubPrevTarget` is updated on
every served tick, so the sign is the drag's own. The operator was dragging
toward the past.

**What WAS broken is the speculation.** `maybeScrubPrefetch` probed the fragment
adjacent to the one just served (`seg.startMs ± durMs`) — correct for a careful
drag that walks fragment to fragment, and correct for nothing else. At 26 s per
tick the neighbour is never visited, so every one of those prefetches spent a
full cold GOP read on bytes nothing would ever ask for: **7 wasted reads out of
15 in the regression test**, issued against the very disk the drag was waiting
on, concurrently with the critical path. And it was invisible — prefetch reads
never reached the census, so the I/O load looked like half what it was.

## Decision

Speculation probes where the drag is HEADED: `target + dir × max(|step|,
durMs + 1) × horizon`, where `step` is the delta between the last two served
targets. The `max` keeps the careful-drag behaviour exactly as it was (a small
step still probes past the fragment edge, clearing the 11–17 ms inter-segment
crack); a large step follows the drag instead of the fragment boundary.

Depth follows NEED, not appetite: more than one horizon is read ahead only when
the step leaves the fragment — a drag with locality is already served by the
memo at zero I/O and must not have its disk load doubled. Concurrency is bounded
by `SCRUB_PREFETCH_MAX_INFLIGHT = 2`, and the direction-run guard is unchanged,
so a reversing drag speculates nothing.

Every speculative read is counted on the drag census — `prefetchReads`,
`prefetchBytes` and, crucially, `prefetchHits`: the ticks actually served by a
fragment speculation landed. Speculation that cannot be shown to pay for itself
is load on the disk the drag is already waiting for.

The fragment cache is bounded in BYTES (`SCRUB_FRAGMENT_CACHE_MB = 2` per WebRTC
session) with a floor of 4 entries, replacing a bound of 8 entries that was
anywhere between 0.3 and 1.4 MB depending on fragment size.

## Consequences

- In the measured regime the ticks' own reads collapse (the regression test goes
  from 8 cold reads in 8 ticks to ≤4) and the wasted reads become useful ones.
  The ceiling stops being one cold read per push and becomes the pipeline depth.
- Speculation can still guess wrong — an accelerating drag will miss. It now
  costs the same reads it always cost, and `prefetchReads` vs `prefetchHits`
  says so out loud instead of hiding it.
- `SCRUB_PREFETCH_MAX_INFLIGHT` is deliberately small. These reads land on the
  spindles the recorder is writing every camera to; past a few, concurrent
  random reads stop overlapping and start thrashing. Raise it against a measured
  `prefetchHits` / `readMsP50` pair.
- 2 MB per session is a fleet cost of `2 MB × concurrent timeline viewers`.
- NOT addressed here, and the next lever if this is not enough: one
  `readGopBytes` still costs an `fs.open` plus 3 reads (a 2 KB tail probe for the
  `mfra` index, the `ftyp`+`moov` head, then the fragment), with no memo of the
  index or the head across calls — `seededIndex` only covers segments this
  process finalized in the last 26 h. On a drag that lands in a different 10 s
  segment file every tick, that is 3 seeks per push on a contended array.

## Enforcement

`packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/recorded/__tests__/scrub-prefetch.spec.ts`
drives the measured regime (26 s steps, 4 s fragments, 10 s segments) and pins:
the next tick's fragment is prefetched, the tick reads collapse, every
speculative read is on the census and reconciles with the reads actually issued,
the careful drag still warms only its neighbour, and a reversing drag spends
nothing. `scrub-fragment-cache.spec.ts` pins the byte bound and its entry floor.
Each was verified to fail with its fix removed.
