## Context

The live-decode ceiling is derived: `floor(nodeDecodeBudgetMb / footprint)`,
clamped by `maxLiveDecodeWorkers`. The footprint was
`budgetMb + tileBudgetMb + 350` — the operator's lease budget taken at face
value as the pixels a worker may hold. It is not. The lease store is built
with `maxFrames: holdFrames` (`decode-worker-child.ts`), and at the shipped
`holdFrames: 8` eight 4K RGB24 frames are ~200 MB: the COUNT binds, the
budget never does. `sceneBudgetMb` was not charged at all.

On 2026-08-28 between 15:19Z and 16:14Z `budgetMb` went back from the
operator's 512 to the shipped 1024, the footprint stepped 926 → 1438 and the
ceiling 8 → 5 on every node, with no intermediate value. The Mac agent
(M2, 16 GB, `detectWeight` 70 — it carries most of the fleet) refused 7,595
decodes in the following day, 98% of the cluster's refusals, across 27
cameras, every refusal carrying the same tuple `ceiling=5 live=5
committedMb=7190 workerFootprintMb=1438`. Measured over the same day, its
workers held `leaseMb` p50 24, max 190, `leaseFrames` max 8; RSS p50 574,
p90 952. `WORKER IS LARGE` (2048 MB) never fired. The refusals were
arithmetic, not memory. CoreML inference on that node ran at 29 ms p50 with
no queue — the bottleneck was admission, and the refusals cost 13,294 native
crop misses and 6,637 `no crop source resolved` on the same day.

## Decision

`decodeWorkerFootprintMb` charges the pixels the store can actually hold —
`min(budgetMb, holdFrames × MAX_HELD_FRAME_MB)` with `MAX_HELD_FRAME_MB = 25`
(one 4K RGB24 frame, measured twice at 23.7) — plus `tileBudgetMb`, plus
`sceneBudgetMb`, plus the baseline. Every knob that moves a worker's worst
case is in the formula; no knob that cannot move it is. Admission does not
need the source resolution: 4K is the ceiling any camera can reach.

## Consequences

At the shipped knobs the footprint is 662 MB and the ceiling on an 8192 MB
budget is the count cap, 8 — the concurrency the cluster had before
2026-08-28. Raising `holdFrames` past `budgetMb / 25` makes the budget bind
again, exactly as before. The baseline (350) is still the hub's spawn floor,
not the Mac's measured plateau (~550 net of lease); `committedMb` therefore
under-reports on that node, and correcting it is a measurement to take, not a
constant to guess. Two things this does NOT do, recorded because they were
found on the way: `nodeDecodeBudgetMb` and `maxLiveDecodeWorkers` are
cluster-wide settings tuned for the 64 GB hub and applied unchanged to the
16 GB Mac; and the load balancer never learns of a refusal, so weight alone
moves refusals between nodes rather than removing them.

## Enforcement

`live-worker-admission.spec.ts`: the footprint at the shipped defaults is
below the budget knob; a larger `budgetMb` changes nothing while the frame
count binds; `holdFrames`, `tileBudgetMb` and `sceneBudgetMb` each move it;
an 8192 MB node admits 8. Removing the `min` fails four tests (verified).
