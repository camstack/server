## Context

The live `stream-broker` runner grew to multiple GB and OOM-killed on the hub —
12 kills in 5 days, most absorbed invisibly by `CrashSupervisor`. `VmRSS` and the
glibc `[heap]` mapping said "native `Buffer` memory", but not which buffer. The
first three suspects (`decoderSeedBuffer`, `rtpRing`, `pendingParamNals`) were all
already bounded, and an attach/detach experiment on `VmRSS` bounded the scale of
one candidate but could not name the retainer.

A `SIGUSR2` heap snapshot from the leaking process settled it: the `[mem]` line's
`arrayBuffers ≈ external ≈ 1.86 GB` proved the bytes were JS-referenced Buffers,
and reverse-edge attribution put **1567 MB in a single field**,
`RtspRestreamer.lastKeyframeRtpPackets`. The cache accumulates one keyframe GOP's
packets and is cleared by a latch that resets only when the *next* keyframe
starts. If a P-frame never arrives to close the latch — a client-less stream that
drops P-frames before the latch, or a genuinely all-intra / post-IDR-stall stream
— the latch re-opens and appends forever. A smarter latch would still be one
unhandled stream shape away from the same failure.

## Decision

A media buffer that fills at frame rate must have a **hard byte cap**, enforced by
a running byte counter reset with the buffer, not merely a correct control-flow
latch. `RtspRestreamer`'s keyframe caches (RTP passthrough and Annex-B twins) cap
at 16 MiB: on overflow, drop the partial GOP and stop collecting until the next
keyframe re-opens it. The cache oscillates below the cap instead of growing
unbounded — memory-safe regardless of stream shape or client state.

## Consequences

- A pathological stream loses its late-joiner keyframe burst (bounded to ≤ the
  cap) rather than the whole broker to OOM. That is the correct trade on an NVR.
- The cap is a backstop, not a substitute for correct latch logic — both stay.
- The same rule applies to any future frame-rate collection (ring buffers,
  reorder queues): bound it in bytes at the point of append, not by reasoning
  about when it "should" drain.

## Enforcement

A gate test in `rtsp-restreamer-live-edge.spec.ts` feeds an all-intra stream that
never closes the latch and asserts the late-joiner burst stays bounded; it fails
with the cap removed (verified per the CLAUDE.md "remove the fix, confirm the test
fails" rule). Otherwise review — there is no lint that knows which buffers are
frame-rate.
