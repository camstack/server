## Context

The decode worker keeps `holdFrames` (8) native frames for the runner's lazy
frame refs and evicts the OLDEST when a newer marked frame lands. The runner's
inference semaphore admits 16 calls at once and its picker has no per-camera
limit. Each number was right on its own. Together, at every fresh session, a
cold engine let sixteen frames of ONE camera into inference; frames 9–16
evicted 1–8 from the hold, and the first eight reads failed with
`toBuffer for unknown or stale frameId` — measured on 2026-09-06 as exactly
eight errors, ids 1–8, in the same second the engine warmed, on every node.
D361's backpressure rule (copy the pixels inline once the semaphore is
exhausted) never fired: sixteen permits were never exhausted by one camera.

## Decision

The runner caps in-flight inferences PER CAMERA at `holdFrames - 1`
(`maxInflightPerCamera`, never below one). A camera at its cap keeps its
latest frame queued until one settles, and `detectionBackpressure` reports
true for it, so the pump copies that frame inline instead of handing out a
ref the hold cannot honour. The wrapper resolves the cap from the same
native-lease setting the worker's hold is sized from and re-applies it on
every lease-settings refresh, so the two numbers cannot drift apart again.

## Consequences

One camera can no longer flood a cold engine with near-identical frames;
the permits it would have held go to the other cameras. Raising
`nativeLeaseHoldFrames` raises the cap; lowering it to 1 serialises that
camera's inferences. The refs a worker is asked for are now always within
its hold, so a remaining `unknown or stale frameId` is a real fault, not
sizing.
