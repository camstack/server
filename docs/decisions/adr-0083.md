## Context

The `device-audio` switch ([D74](adr-0074.md)) shipped on 2026-08-06 and spent
one day in production. It works, and its first day produced two complaints that
are the same complaint from two ends.

**It is the vendor's switch, not ours.** Turning it off is an ISAPI or Baichuan
write that restarts the camera's encoders. Measured on 104: 25.5 s sequential
for three channels, ~4.3 s after D77 parallelised them, and every one of those
seconds is a stream interruption. It also only exists where the vendor exposes
it — a camera whose firmware has no microphone control cannot be silenced at
all, and `getOptions().supportsAudioMute === false` renders no switch, which is
honest and useless. The operator asked for a way to silence a camera that is
instant, works on every camera, and does not touch the camera.

**And the recovery from that restart is a single event.** D77's chain is
provider emits `StreamParamsChanged` → `redialDeviceSources` → the sessions
re-sync. It is correct, it was verified live, and it is one event — which
[D8](adr-0008.md) says plainly is telemetry and may be dropped. Two things walk
past it: a lost event, and an audio change made on the camera's own web UI,
which emits nothing at all. Either way the broker keeps a video-only session
against a camera that is muxing audio, `hasDemand()` is satisfied,
`scheduleReconnect` has no reason to fire, and nothing asks again. That is the
state that left 104 deaf for forty minutes, and the only cure available was
disabling the device for longer than the catalog reconcile.

The obvious build for the first half is to widen `device-audio` — have one
switch write the camera when it can and the broker when it cannot. That is
wrong for the reason D62 gives: it would be one control reporting two different
facts, and an operator reading "off" would not know whether the camera is
deaf or merely quiet. Hardware privacy and distribution policy are different
promises.

## Decision

**They are two switches, and neither reads the other's store.**

`device-audio` writes the CAMERA (`privacyMask.setAudioEnabled`): hardware
privacy, vendor-dependent, survives CamStack entirely, costs an encoder
restart. `broker-audio` writes THIS SERVER: the broker distributes none of the
device's audio, instantly, on every camera, while the camera keeps capturing.

[D62](adr-0062.md) forbids a second switch that DISAGREES with the first. These
cannot: the camera holds one fact, the broker holds the other, each row reports
its own, and `CameraStatus.audio.enabled` is false when EITHER is off (plus
`audio-analysis` — three switches silence that block, and a list rebuilt at the
call site is a list that will miss the next one).

Four things about the mute are not optional:

1. **It stores nothing new.** The authority is `DeviceOverride.audioMuted`, in
   the blob the broker already persists under the `deviceOverrides` store key
   alongside `preBuffer`, `streamingDebug` and `hwaccel`. Per DEVICE, never per
   camStream: every stream of a camera carries the same microphone, so a
   per-stream value could only let main and sub disagree about whether the
   camera is silent.

2. **BOTH planes, and they are muted at different layers** (operator decision,
   2026-08-07). Live rides `onEncodedData`, so the guard is on the
   encoded-packet fan-out. Recording rides the RTSP restreamer the recorder's
   ffmpeg pulls — and dropping only its RTP would be worse than not muting:
   `buildPassthroughArgs` uses `-map 0`, so ffmpeg maps whatever the SDP
   declared at CONNECT time, and an `m=audio` we have decided never to fill
   leaves the mp4 muxer holding video up to `max_interleave_delta`. So while
   muted the restreamer serves the video-only SDP (the `/muted` variant that
   already existed for local decoders) to EVERY puller, and the transition
   closes the audio-bearing pulls once so they re-`DESCRIBE`. The recorder's
   `SegmentWriter` respawns with bounded backoff; the cost is one segment
   boundary per flip.

3. **The mute is applied where audio ENTERS, and re-checked where it LEAVES.**
   Ingress guards (`onAudioRtp` before the depacketizer, `pushEncodedPacket`
   before the codec session) mean nothing is decoded in order to be discarded.
   The `fanOutEncoded` guard is what makes "no audio is distributed" a property
   rather than a probability: a decode session or the Opus encoder can still
   emit a unit queued before the mute landed. One boolean per packet.

4. **It is applied to a broker at CREATION, before `start()`.** A mute that
   only touched live brokers would test green all day and lapse silently on the
   next respawn, reconnect or catalog republish.

**And D77's recovery becomes a reconcile, with the event kept as the fast
path.** Every catalog-reconcile tick compares desired against observed, per
device — but the two halves cost wildly different amounts, so the cheap
question comes first ([D54](adr-0054.md)):

| | Question | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OBSERVED | do my streaming source brokers carry an audio track? | free, in-memory |
| DESIRED | does the camera declare audio enabled? | an ISAPI/Baichuan round trip |

A camera delivering audio is a camera whose audio is enabled, so the device is
only ever asked when the free half already looks wrong. A steady-state fleet
pays nothing, and a broker-muted camera is never asked at all — silence is what
was requested.

The decision itself is a pure function (`audio-plane-reconcile.ts`) because its
only action tears down a HEALTHY reader, which is the destructive direction
[D49](adr-0049.md) is about: a disagreement must be seen on **two** consecutive
passes before it is believed, a read that FAILED changes nothing (it does not
clear an existing disagreement either — one flaky round trip must not launder a
real one), re-dials are spaced by a 5-minute cooldown, capped at 3, and past the
cap the device is declared unrecoverable ONCE rather than re-dialled forever.
Any agreeing observation clears the memory completely, re-dial budget included:
the cap exists to stop a hopeless loop, not to ration a working camera.

## Consequences

- **Recordings made while muted are silent forever**, and the cost line says so
  in those words. It is the only row in the group whose OFF destroys something
  that turning it back on cannot restore — recording's OFF keeps the footage
  already on disk; this one makes the footage recorded from now permanently
  mute.
- **The mute starves audio analysis**, exactly as the camera switch does. That
  is why `CameraStatus.audio.enabled` had to learn about all three switches:
  reporting `enabled: true` while the broker distributes nothing would
  reintroduce the hardcoded lie D62 removed from that field once already.
- **Flipping it costs one RTSP re-pull**, so one recording segment boundary and
  a blip for any live RTSP consumer. Deliberate, and the alternative (leaving
  the `m=audio` in place) is a muxer stall.
- **`broker-audio` is the only row in the group that is NODE-PINNED.** The
  stream-broker cap is a per-node singleton and a camera's brokers live on its
  source owner, so `CameraSwitchService` gained an `assignSource` dep — the same
  pin `CameraStatusService` already uses for every stream-broker call. An
  unpinned read would answer for the hub's brokers about a camera it does not
  serve.
- **The row is offered whenever the broker answers**, including on a camera
  that is currently silent. That is not the privacy-mask `not-configured` case:
  flipping the mask master with zero zones is provably a no-op forever, whereas
  the mute is a policy governing what happens when sound appears.
- **The reconcile can re-dial a camera nobody asked it to.** Bounded above, and
  every branch — including the ones that change nothing — logs with
  `tags: { deviceId }`. A reconcile whose "I looked and everything was fine" is
  unobservable is one nobody can trust after the next incident.
- **`BrokerStats` gained `audioMuted` + `audioMutedDropped`**, in BOTH the Zod
  schema and the hand-written `BrokerStats` interface. Those two are not
  generated from each other; a field added to one alone compiles and then
  disappears on the wire.
- **Push sources were already silent on the recording plane** and this does not
  change it: `pushEncodedPacket` gates the restreamer on `packet.type ===
  'video'`, so a Reolink Baichuan camera's audio never reached it. Known,
  unrelated, not fixed here.
- **The transcode-egress audio sidecar pushes into a DIFFERENT restreamer
  instance.** Its own `pushAudioRtpPassthrough` guard covers the packets; a
  derived stream's grafted `m=audio` block is covered because the manager
  applies the mute to derived brokers too.

## Enforcement

- `packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/__tests__/audio-plane-reconcile.spec.ts`
  — the pure decision, 17 specs: agreement clears the budget, a failed read
  changes nothing and does not launder a disagreement, one pass never acts, the
  cooldown holds, and 60 disagreeing passes produce exactly 3 re-dials and
  exactly 1 give-up.
- `packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/__tests__/broker-audio-mute.spec.ts`
  — both planes: audio stops and video does not, the drop is announced once and
  carries `tags: { deviceId }`, the restreamer serves the video-only SDP to
  every puller, only audio-bearing pulls are closed, and the mute survives
  `resetStreamState`. Verified discriminating — with the ingress guard alone
  removed all 12 still pass (the fan-out guard carries the property); with both
  removed 4 fail; with the `getSdp` branch removed 3 fail.
- `packages/addon-pipeline/src/stream-broker/__tests__/device-audio-mute-manager.spec.ts`
  — persistence into the existing override blob with every sibling field
  intact, application to a broker created AFTER the mute (removing
  `applyAudioMuteConfig` fails exactly that one), and the reconcile's
  cheap-first gates (removing either makes the camera be read when it must not
  be).
- `packages/types/src/interfaces/__tests__/camera-switches.spec.ts` — the
  derivation: polarity, `source-unreachable` for a broker node that did not
  answer, the two audio rows reporting independently, and the cost line naming
  "forever".
- `packages/addon-pipeline-orchestrator/src/__tests__/camera-switch-service.spec.ts`
  — routing: the write goes to the broker and touches no other authority, least
  of all the camera; the two stores stay independent across a flip of each.
- `packages/addon-pipeline-orchestrator/src/__tests__/camera-status-audio-enabled.spec.ts`
  — all three silencing switches, plus the inverse (an unrelated switch off
  leaves audio enabled). Its fake previously filtered `switchedOffIds` down to
  `audio-analysis`; passing it through verbatim is part of this change, because
  the filter would have hidden the whole record.

The exhaustive `switch` over `CameraSwitchAuthority` remains the compiler's
share: `broker-audio-mute` could not be added without wiring both `resolveState`
and `applyWrite`.
