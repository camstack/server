# INT8 recipe: keep the YOLO Detect head in float — researched, measured, shipped

Measured 2026-10-08 → 10-09. Accuracy was measured offline on a developer Mac,
latency on the cluster's own hardware: the hub's Core Ultra 7 265K (NPU and
iGPU) and the N100 agent's iGPU and CPU. The cluster runs were short and
read-only. They executed in each node's container Python against IR copies
under `/tmp`; no camera, pipeline or setting was touched. Hub frames never left
the Mac. Decision record: [D818](../decisions/adr-0818-int8-keeps-the-yolo-detect-head-in-float.md).

## Why

The 2026-10-08 study ([int8-recalibration](2026-10-08-int8-recalibration.md))
found that the hosted OpenVINO INT8 YOLO IRs lose mAP50-95 against their float
base while mAP50 barely moves. That is a loss of box precision. The fleet's
live `yolov9m-640-int8` (hub NPU + iGPU) lost 0.07 on COCO and 0.11 on hub
frames. The hypothesis to test here: quantising the box-regression / DFL head
hurts localisation.

**Result: confirmed, and fixed.**

- With the head's decode in float, every YOLOv9 INT8 IR is within noise of
  float on both sets.
  - `yolov9m-640`: COCO 0.503 → 0.571 (float 0.573), hub 0.632 → 0.748 (float
    0.744).
  - The size is unchanged, and NPU latency is within 1 %.
- Every YOLO26 IR gains 0.007–0.037 mAP50-95 with the whole head in float. That costs
  about 15–21 % in size, and 0–6 % on the NPU but up to 22 % on the iGPU.
- **14 of the 21 IRs were replaced on the mirror. 7 YOLO26 IRs were kept**,
  because the float head was more than 10 % slower on the iGPU.

## What the published recipes do

Every source below keeps the YOLO Detect head's **decode** in float. Our recipe
before this study did not: `convert_lib.quantize_openvino_int8` called
`nncf.quantize(model, dataset, subset_size=N)` with every default (preset
PERFORMANCE, fast bias correction, no ignored scope).

- **Ultralytics `export(format="openvino", int8=True)`** —
  `ultralytics/utils/export/openvino.py` [U4]. For every Detect subclass it
  keeps the head module's own ops and the DFL in float, and every `Sigmoid`. The
  conv branches (`cv2`/`cv3`) stay INT8. It quantises with
  `preset=QuantizationPreset.MIXED` over the whole calibration set:
  ```python
  ignored_scope = nncf.IgnoredScope(names=[op.get_friendly_name() for op in ops
      if op.get_type_name() == "Sigmoid"
      or op.get_friendly_name().startswith((f"{head}/", f"{head}.dfl"))])
  nncf.quantize(ov_model, ds, preset=nncf.QuantizationPreset.MIXED,
                subset_size=len(ds), ignored_scope=ignored_scope)
  ```
  - The head exclusion arrived in PR #7516 / #8061 [U3][U5], which moved
    YOLOv8n from 35.7 to 36.3 mAP50-95.
  - The DFL was added in #25306 [U1], which calls the DFL softmax "highly
    sensitive to INT8 rounding".
  - With the full recipe, Ultralytics measures INT8 at −0.002 to −0.005
    mAP50-95 from FP32 on COCO: YOLOv8n, YOLOv9t, YOLO11n [U1]. YOLO26 loses
    0.011–0.015 [U2].
- **OpenVINO notebooks** [O1–O4].
  - The YOLOv8 notebook ignores a head subgraph and uses MIXED.
  - The YOLOv9/v10/v11 notebooks use MIXED alone.
  - The YOLO26 notebook and NNCF's own YOLO26 example [O3][N2] use:
    ```python
    nncf.quantize(model, ds, subset_size=len(ds), preset=nncf.QuantizationPreset.MIXED,
                  fast_bias_correction=False, ignored_scope=nncf.IgnoredScope(patterns=[".*one2one.*"]))
    ```
    NNCF's CI reference for it is −0.006 mAP [N3].
- **Accuracy control** [N4][O4]: `nncf.quantize_with_accuracy_control` with a
  mAP validation function.
  - NNCF's YOLOv8 example uses `max_drop=0.003`, MIXED, and the head ignored.
  - The YOLO11 notebook uses `max_drop=0.01` and `ranking_subset_size=25`. The
    first op it reverts to float is the head's stride `Multiply`.
- **`nncf.quantize` options** [N1][N5]:
  - Weights are per-channel symmetric by default. Activations are per-tensor,
    range MEAN_MINMAX.
  - **MIXED** makes activations asymmetric, which fits SiLU outputs.
  - `fast_bias_correction=False` runs the full BiasCorrection.
  - `AdvancedQuantizationParameters` covers SmoothQuant, range estimators
    (MEAN_QUANTILE, …), `overflow_fix` and per-channel switches. SmoothQuant
    matters for transformers, not convolutional YOLO.
  - In NNCF's CPU hardware config, `Add`/`Multiply`/`Subtract`/`Divide` take
    8-bit per-tensor activation quantisers.
- **NPU** (Intel Core Ultra, the NPU plugin docs) [D1]:
  - The NPU runs U8 "quantised models: INT8 or mixed FP16-INT8". "Computation
    precision for the HW is FP16."
  - An op whose FakeQuantize was removed therefore runs in FP16 **on the NPU**.
    There is no automatic CPU fallback: that would need HETERO/AUTO, which the
    pool does not use.
  - Only static shapes are supported.
- **iGPU** [D2]: u8/i8 is used for quantised ops only. Every other op runs in
  the IR's float precision (FP16 by default). Mixed IRs are supported.

**Why the decode hurts.** In the YOLOv9 head, the hosted IRs carry
FakeQuantize ops on these head-own ops:

- `/model.22/Sub`, `/model.22/Add_1`, `/model.22/Sub_1` and `/model.22/Div_1`:
  the anchor ± distance, the box centre and the box size, in grid units;
- `/model.22/dfl/Softmax`.

So box coordinates sit on a 256-level grid before the ×stride `Mul`. Over a
0–80 cell range that is about 0.3 cells, which is 2.5–10 px at strides 8–32.
That explains the measured loss: mAP50 holds, mAP50-95 collapses.

## How it was measured

This uses the same harness, sets and rule as the 2026-10-08 study:
`scripts/eval/evaluate_variants.py`, the pool's own PrePostProcessor and YOLO
decode, floor 0.05, NMS 0.45.

- **COCO:** val2017 images 301–800 in file-name order. These are disjoint from
  the calibration images (the first 300 or 128).
- **Hub:** 400 stored `keyFrameSmall` frames with the YOLO26-X@1280 AND
  RT-DETR-X@640 pseudo ground truth.
- **Baseline:** the IR the mirror serves today, called "hosted". Its `.bin`
  sha256 was checked against `hosted-model-revisions.ts` and against the
  mirror itself before the replacement.
- **Float:** the catalog's base IR of the same model.
- **Replacement rule:** paired bootstrap (1000 resamples). A candidate is
  replaced only if none of the 16 differences is a significant drop. The 16
  are AP50 and AP50-95, for the mean and for person, vehicle and animal, on
  both sets.

**INT8 execution.** Every variant ran as exact FP32 fake-quantisation on the
Mac: OpenVINO 2026.2.1 on ARM with `LP_TRANSFORMS_MODE=false`. All variants of
a model ran on that same path. The emulation was checked against real
hardware (below), and it matches every real device within 0.003 mAP50-95.

**Calibration.** The same as the hosted IRs:

- COCO val2017, the first 300 images (YOLOv9) or 128 (YOLO26), in file-name
  order;
- centred letterbox;
- OpenVINO 2026.2.1 + NNCF 3.2.0;
- the **hosted ONNX** (`--reuse-onnx`).

The final IRs were built by `scripts/build-camstack-models.py` with the new
`convert_lib`. Each is bit-identical (`.bin` sha256) to the study IR that was
measured, for all 21.

### Variants

All variants use the decode scope below:

```python
H = "/model\\.22"                 # the Detect module: the module of the graph's last Sigmoid
decode = [rf"{H}/[^/]+$", rf"{H}/dfl/"]   # the head's OWN ops + the DFL — NNCF matches with re.match (prefix), so anchor
```

| label | ignored scope | preset | bias correction | N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hosted | none | PERFORMANCE (default) | fast | 300 / 128 |
| decode | head's own ops + DFL | PERFORMANCE | fast | same |
| box | decode + the box conv branch (`cv2.*`) | PERFORMANCE | fast | same |
| head | the whole Detect module, `{H}/.*` | PERFORMANCE | fast | same |
| ultra | decode (the Ultralytics exporter's scope) | **MIXED** | fast | same |
| **ultra+nobc** (YOLOv9 recipe) | decode | MIXED | **full** | same |
| ultra+nobc+q | decode | MIXED | full, activations MEAN_QUANTILE | same |
| ultra+nobc-n1000 | decode | MIXED | full | 1000 (COCO 0–299 + 1500–2199) |
| **head+mixed+nobc** (YOLO26 recipe) | the whole Detect module | MIXED | full | same |
| ac-base | `quantize_with_accuracy_control`, max_drop 0.01 ABSOLUTE | default | fast | same |

**Accuracy control.** The validation function is the mean over person, vehicle
and animal of AP50-95 on COCO val2017 images 1001–1500, which are disjoint
from both calibration and test. Ranking is by that metric on a 150-image
subset, with at most 30 iterations.

What this shows:

- **The decode is the loss.** Taking the head's own ops and the DFL out of
  INT8 recovers 89–94 % of the float gap on `yolov9m-640` and 57–77 % on
  `yolov9s-320`. That is five FakeQuantize ops:
  - `/model.22/Sub` and `Add_1`: anchor ± distance;
  - `Sub_1` and `Div_1`: the box centre and size;
  - `dfl/Softmax`.

  Keeping the box conv branch float as well (**box**) adds nothing. Keeping the
  whole head float (**head**) adds little, and costs 11 % NPU latency on
  `yolov9m-640` (22.7 vs 20.3 ms).
- **MIXED + full bias correction closes the rest.** With them, both models
  match float on both sets.
  - The quantile range estimator (**+q**) and 1000 calibration images
    (**-n1000**) add nothing beyond noise. The quantile estimator also costs a
    significant hub-animal AP50 drop on `yolov9m-640`.
  - **ultra+nobc** is the recipe: the Ultralytics exporter's scope and preset,
    plus the full bias correction from NNCF's own YOLO26 example.
- **Accuracy control:**
  - With **NMSE ranking** (validation returns raw outputs), NNCF reverted
    backbone convs first (`/model.1`, `/model.2`, `/model.4`), and the drop
    stayed at 0.020 after 5 reverts.
  - With **metric ranking**, the first group it reverted was the decode
    (`/model.22/Sub_1`, `/model.22/Add_2`). That halved the drop to 0.010.
    Then `/model.0` brought it under the 0.01 target, after 75 min on
    `yolov9s-320`.
  - The resulting IR is better than hosted but below **ultra+nobc** on both
    sets. Here accuracy control found the same culprit on its own, but a fixed
    scope is cheaper and better.
  - **On `yolov9m-640` it did not find the cause.** This run used metric
    ranking on a 100-image subset, at most 10 iterations. The validation drop
    started at 0.125 (float 0.560, INT8 0.435). The ranking put the first
    backbone convs on top: `/model.0`, `/model.1`, `/model.2/cv1`. After
    three reverts the drop was still 0.122–0.126. The run was stopped after
    50 min, so there is no IR to evaluate.

### NPU compile

**Every static IR compiles and runs entirely on the NPU.** That covers all 8
YOLOv9 IRs and the 5 YOLO26 @640 IRs. `EXECUTION_DEVICES` reports `NPU`, with
no HETERO and no CPU fallback. This is the documented behaviour: ops whose
FakeQuantize was removed run in FP16 on the NPU itself [D1].

The recipe's raw output also agrees with the CPU better than hosted's does:

- `yolov9m-640`: cosine 0.99997 vs 0.99979 on the NPU;
- every YOLOv9: ≥ 0.9993 on NPU and iGPU.

**The reduced YOLO26 IRs (`-320`, `-256`) cannot run on the NPU, hosted or
recipe.** They have dynamic spatial axes. The pool already says so ("DYNAMIC
NON-BATCH axis … this compile is expected to fail"), and a direct NPU compile
aborts the process. Their only accelerated target is the iGPU, so they were
judged on the iGPU.

### A correction to the 2026-10-08 study

The 2026-10-08 study ran 9 of its 21 INT8 models as real x86 INT8 kernels
**under Rosetta 2**. It cross-checked that path against emulation only on
`yolov9m-640`, where they agree (0.501 vs 0.503 here). On some small models
that path is badly off:

| model | Rosetta 2026-10-08 | Emulation (this study) | Real device |
|---|---|---|---|
| `yolov9t-320` | 0.283 | 0.343 | 0.343–0.345 on the hub CPU, NPU and iGPU |
| `yolov9t-640` | 0.365 | 0.419 | – |
| `yolo26s-320` | 0.369 | 0.429 | – |

(all hosted IR, COCO mAP50-95)

The likely cause: Rosetta exposes no AVX-VNNI/AVX-512, so OpenVINO runs its
non-VNNI INT8 kernels. Their u8×s8 products saturate at 16 bits, and NNCF's
overflow fix covers only the first layer by default. No node runs that path:
the 265K and the N100 both have AVX-VNNI, and the real 265K CPU matches the
emulation.

So for those rows the absolute "INT8 cost" in the 2026-10-08 study is
overstated. For example, `yolov9t-320` loses 0.017 to float, not 0.077. Its
old-vs-new verdicts still stand: both sides ran on the same path.

This study uses the emulated path throughout, and checks it on real devices.

## Decision

The replacement rule had three conditions:

1. accuracy improves beyond noise;
2. none of the 16 differences is a significant drop;
3. latency is within ~10 % on the target device, and the NPU still runs the IR.

**Target device:**

- the device the model runs on in the fleet (`yolov9m-640`: hub NPU + iGPU;
  `yolov9s-320`: N100);
- otherwise the hub NPU and iGPU;
- the iGPU alone for the dynamic-shape reduced YOLO26 IRs.

The latency cut is a median per-round ratio of at most 1.10.

**Replaced: 14.**

- All 8 YOLOv9: `yolov9{t,s,m,c}-{320,640}-int8`.
- 6 YOLO26: `yolo26{n,s,l}-int8`, `yolo26n-320-int8`, `yolo26{n,s}-256-int8`.

**Kept: 7.** These are the YOLO26 IRs whose float head costs 11–22 % on the
iGPU: `yolo26m-int8`, `yolo26x-int8`, `yolo26{s,m,l}-320-int8` and
`yolo26{m,l}-256-int8`.

- All 7 improve 0.007–0.037 mAP50-95 with no significant drop, so the operator
  may prefer accuracy here.
- `yolo26m`, `yolo26x` and `yolo26l-320` sit at 1.11–1.12, right at the edge.
- The recipe IRs are kept locally in `int8-recipe-work/final/` for that call.

On YOLOv9, `yolov9s-320` is 15 % slower on the hub iGPU. It is replaced
because its target is the N100: +5.5 % over 20 rounds there, and the NPU is
unchanged.

### D739 floors

The D739 rule was applied to the hub set, as in 2026-10-08:

- the root `yolov9m-320-int8` (hosted) at 0.65 / 0.50 / 0.50 sets the
  false-positive budget;
- each model gets the lowest 0.05-grid floor within it.

A shipped floor was changed only when a class moved **beyond the ±0.10 noise**
the floor record documents. Three models qualify:

| model | class | hosted → recipe (measured) | shipped before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `yolov9m-320-int8` | animal | 0.50 → 0.35 | 0.45 → **0.35** |
| `yolov9s-640-int8` | animal | 0.45 → 0.60 | 0.55 → **0.60** |
| `yolo26l-int8` | person / vehicle / animal | 0.70 / 0.55 / 0.50 → 0.50 / 0.40 / 0.40 | global 0.65 / 0.50 / 0.50 → **0.50 / 0.40 / 0.40** (now a measured row) |

Every other replaced model moved by 0.10 or less on every class, so it keeps
its floor. `model-floors.json` records the three changes. Each changed class
carries a `remeasured` note: one run, 400 frames, emulated CPU. The original
rows were medians over CPU, NPU and GPU runs on 409 frames.
