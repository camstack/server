# agent-a N100 iGPU — concurrent detection capacity (2026-10-24)

covers:
  - packages/addon-pipeline/src/pipeline-runner/node-bench/**
  - packages/addon-pipeline/python/inference_pool.py
applies-to:
  - packages/addon-pipeline/src/pipeline-runner/node-bench/node-bench-driver.ts
  - packages/addon-pipeline/python/inference_pool.py
last-verified: 2026-10-24

Question: how many cameras does agent-a's N100 iGPU (`openvino:gpu`)
hold, per model and per detection fps? The 2026-10-24 single-request table
([2026-10-24-agent-a-after-vaapi.md](2026-10-24-agent-a-after-vaapi.md))
turned warm medians into `floor(1000 / p50 / fps)` and called that the camera
count. The pool runs OpenVINO with `PERFORMANCE_HINT=LATENCY` and at least two
InferRequests (`OV_MIN_CONCURRENCY` in `inference_pool.py`). This page measures
concurrent streams on the same device.

## Method

D791 node benchmark, device phase only (root detector, no detail steps), on
`agent-a` / `openvino:gpu`. Runs `nb-mvmblavv` (10 fps) and
`nb-mvmbojq7` (5 fps). The orchestrator plan offers a chosen model list and
`fpsPerStream`; it does not offer the ramp or the window. What ran:

- Frames decoded once into RAM (`camstack-bench-v1`, 114 frames, 640×360).
  Session decode and VAAPI are not in these numbers (D791: "not measured:
  session decode").
- Separate benchmark pool (D910), not the live detection pool. Fingerprint on
  every cell: `runtime openvino`, `numWorkers 1`, `concurrency 1`,
  `batchMode none`. That `concurrency` is the Python dispatch width in
  `runtimes.ts` (OpenVINO keeps its own InferRequest pool; the comment there
  has been `concurrency 1` since 2026-06-24).
- Ramp `1, 2, 4, 6, 8, …` (`DEVICE_RAMP`). Steps 3 and 5 do not exist.
  `stopOnFirstFail` is on, so the first failing step is the last row.
- Each step: 5 s settle (not counted) + 20 s measure. The plan cannot ask for
  the 2-minute window; `measureSec` defaults to 20 and is not a field of
  `startNodeBenchmark`.
- A step passes the harness when e2e p95 ≤ the frame interval (100 ms at
  10 fps, 200 ms at 5 fps) and drops+sheds ≤ 1 %. The task's hold rule used
  below is stricter on delivered rate and looser on a 1 % drop: every stream
  ≥ 95 % of the offered fps, no sustained drops, p95 inside one frame
  interval. Per-stream fps is the cell's aggregate completed fps divided by
  the stream count. p50 is stored only for the single-stream step; later
  steps have p95 only.
- `contaminated: false` on every stored cell. The node's drain did not move
  a camera that was already placed (`rebalance` returned `migrated 0`), so
  those cameras were pinned to other nodes for the run and unpinned
  afterwards. Nothing in `inferenceDevices` was written. After the runs,
  `openvino:gpu` was still `yolov9t-640-int8`, `maxSessions` 3, `cpu`
  disabled.

`intel_gpu_top` engine columns (RCS/BCS/VCS/VECS) stayed at 0 % on every 1 s
sample, including while RC6 and frequency showed the GPU awake — the same
gap as the VAAPI latency note. GPU state below is RC6, requested frequency
and GPU power from the last 1 s sample of each host poll. Container memory
is `docker stats` (cgroup), not RssAnon. Host RAM is MemAvailable out of
15.4 GiB.

A 2-minute live always-on check with two cameras pinned to the node did not
reach a steady state (inference still hundreds of milliseconds, a third
camera landed, and the load line reported an `openvino:npu` attach on a node
whose probed devices are `cpu` and `openvino:gpu`). Those samples are not
in the matrix. Both cameras were put back to `on-motion` at 10 fps and
every pin this session wrote was cleared.

## Does `1000/p50` under-state capacity?

The single-stream p50 from this ramp, read as a serial pipeline, gives
`floor((1000/p50) / fps)` cameras: t-640 → 2 at 10 fps and 4 at 5 fps;
s-640 → 1 at 10 fps and 2 at 5 fps. The 2026-10-24 latency table (50 ms and
81 ms, measured with a camera live) gives the same integers.

Against the ramp:

- At **10 fps** the formula matches what held. t-640 holds 2 and fails 4.
  Three streams were not stepped; they would offer 30 fps, and the failing
  4-stream step only completed 30.5 fps, so 3 is on the throughput ceiling
  rather than a hidden extra camera.
- At **5 fps** the formula under-states t-640 by **2 cameras** (4 predicted,
  6 held). The frame interval is 200 ms and the 6-stream p95 was 75 ms, so
  the latency gate still passes after the serial count is exceeded. s-640
  matches the formula (2).
- Aggregate throughput saturates near **30–32 fps** for t-640 (30.5 fps
  delivered at the failing 10 fps step, 32 fps at the failing 5 fps step)
  against a serial `1000/p50` of ~23 fps — about **1.4×** the serial fps.
  That extra throughput becomes extra cameras only when the frame interval
  is wide enough to hide the queue. It does not become a third 10 fps camera.

## Recommendation

Keep **`yolov9t-640-int8`**, set **`maxSessions` to 2**, leave detection at
**10 fps**.

That is the last 10 fps step that held, on the model the node already runs.
`maxSessions` 3 (what is stored today) admits a third camera that this ramp
cannot show as inside the gate. `yolov9s-640-int8` buys person recall
0.823 against 0.646 and holds two cameras only at 5 fps; at 10 fps it does
not hold one. `yolov9s-320-int8` is the historical capacity model (4 streams
at 10 fps on 2026-10-10, recall 0.438) and it SIGSEGVs the benchmark worker
today, so it is not a candidate until that load is fixed.

## More InferRequests under LATENCY

Not run. There is no env switch for the request count. `OV_MIN_CONCURRENCY`
is 2 and `_OV_PERF_HINT` is `LATENCY`. LATENCY compiles one execution stream;
the second request overlaps preprocess with that stream, which is the modest
1.4× above `1000/p50` already visible here. Raising the pool to 4 requests
under the same hint would not add a second GPU stream. It would keep more
compiled requests resident. `THROUGHPUT` was removed on 2026-10-06 because
that hint multiplies streams and was an N100 OOM-freeze factor. The expected
effect of 4 requests at LATENCY is a small or zero gain on the ~30 fps
ceiling, plus more GPU memory, not a doubling of the camera count.
